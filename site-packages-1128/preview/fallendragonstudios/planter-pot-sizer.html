The rule, in words

Nothing in the sizer is hidden. These four rules are all it knows.

How wide the next pot is

Slow growers and succulents move into a pot 1 to 2 in (2.5 to 5 cm) wider than the one they are in. Fast growers move into a pot 2 to 4 in (5 to 10 cm) wider. This is the gardener's rule of thumb, not a law of nature: it keeps fresh mix within reach of the roots.

Which studio planter fits

Our planters are 5, 6½ and 8 in wide inside. One fits when its width falls inside your range. If two fall inside, the sizer names the one nearest the middle of the range.

How much potting mix

The volume of a cylinder: π × radius × radius × depth, using the new pot's inside width and depth. One US dry quart is 67.2 cubic inches, and one cubic inch is 0.0164 liters. A real pot tapers, so read the figure as the most you will need.

What the sizer will not guess

A width under 1 in or over 16 in, a height outside 1 to 20 in, or anything that is not a number. It says so in plain words and waits for a figure it can use.

How to measure

Width. Lay a ruler across the top of the pot and read the distance between the inside edges of the rim. Not the outside, and not the saucer.

Height. From the floor of the pot to the rim. With the plant still in it, measure the outside and take off a quarter inch for the floor.

Plant type. If the plant has put out several new leaves in the last two months, call it fast. If you cannot remember its last new leaf, call it slow.

Why the next pot is only a little bigger

A pot that is far too large holds a wide ring of wet mix that no root has reached yet. That mix stays wet for days, and the roots beside it rot. One step up gives the roots fresh mix they can fill within a season. Succulents take the smallest step because they use water slowly; a fast grower fills a bigger step within months.

Signs a plant is ready

  • Roots circle the surface or poke out of the drainage hole.
  • Water runs straight through and out, because the pot is mostly root.
  • The plant tips over, or the mix has shrunk away from the wall.
  • It is spring, and the plant has not moved pots in two years.

Repotting, step by step

  1. Water the day beforeA damp root ball slides out whole; a dry one crumbles.
  2. Cover the drainage holeA curved shard or a square of mesh keeps the mix in and lets water out.
  3. Set the heightAdd mix until the top of the root ball sits an inch below the rim of the new pot.
  4. Loosen the rootsTease out any that circle the ball, so they grow outward instead of around.
  5. Fill and firmPour mix into the ring around the ball and press it lightly with your fingers. Do not pack it.
  6. Water throughWater until it runs into the saucer, tip the saucer out, and keep the plant out of hot sun for a week.
