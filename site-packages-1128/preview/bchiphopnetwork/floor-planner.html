The rules it uses

These are rules of thumb, the kind any crew captain carries in their head. The planner applies them in this order, and you can check its answer against them.

  1. Going down

    One dancer going down needs about 2 × 2 m. Power moves get that in one piece, the roll-out floor, so there is no join under a spin. Up to five dancers take turns on one.

  2. Footwork needs less

    Footwork needs less than power: about 1.5 × 1.5 m, which is one pack of four panels. Three to five dancers drilling footwork get two packs clicked into a 1.5 × 3 m strip, so two can go down side by side.

  3. The cypher

    A crew of 6 to 10 cyphers on a circle about 3 m across: one in the middle, the rest round the edge. That is the cypher circle floor.

  4. Big crews

    From eleven dancers up there is still one cypher, so still one circle. The planner adds a second floor for warming up, a roll-out for power or a panel pack for footwork, if your space takes both side by side.

  5. Fit

    The floor has to lie flat inside your space: 3 × 3 m for the circle, 2 × 2 m for the roll-out, 1.5 × 1.5 m for one panel pack and 1.5 × 3 m for two. If the first pick does not fit, the planner steps down to the biggest floor that does, and tells you.

  6. Elbow room

    Less than half a metre clear on a side earns a warning, because legs travel past the edge of a floor.

  7. The split

    Total = items + Original Delivery for your province, charged once per order. Rocket Pool share = total ÷ crew size, rounded up to the next cent.

The guide

How to choose a floor for your crew and space

Start with the spot, not the catalogue. A floor only works where it can lie flat, so the place you practise settles most of the choice before price comes into it.

A park or a court

Painted courts and smooth plazas are the easiest base: flat, wide and open to the sky. Sweep the patch you will use, and keep the roll indoors between sessions, because a floor left out in the rain picks up whatever the rain brings.

A garage or a parkade

A concrete slab is flat and dry, which is most of what a floor asks for. It is also dusty, so sweep twice. Look up before you measure: the ceiling has to clear a handstand.

A community hall

Sealed wood underfoot and a roof overhead. Ask what the hall allows on its boards; a roll-out lies on top with no tape and no glue, which is usually the answer they are hoping for.

  1. Measure the short side first

    The short side decides everything. A 3 m circle needs 3 m both ways; a room 2.8 m wide and 9 m long still cannot take it.

  2. Count who dances at once

    A crew of eight does not need eight floors. One dancer goes down at a time in a cypher, so the floor is sized for the cypher, not the head count.

  3. Be straight about power or footwork

    If windmills are next month's goal, plan for power now. A roll-out is happy under footwork; panels are not happy under windmills.

  4. Leave elbow room

    Half a metre clear on every side is the comfortable minimum. Legs travel further than the floor does.

  5. Think about the carry

    Nine kilos on a shoulder is fine for a few blocks. Sixteen kilos in two rolls wants two people, or a car.

Crew orders

Start a Rocket Pool for your crew

A Rocket Pool is what we call a crew order: one floor that several dancers buy together and all get to use. The name is ours. A floor that is hard for one person to justify gets off the ground quickly when eight people each take a share.

The arithmetic is the last rule on this page. Add up the items, add Original Delivery once, divide by the number in the crew and round up to the cent. The planner does it as you type and the order form does it again, so the number in the crew chat and the number on the order always match.

It is one order from start to finish. One member sends the form, one payment link goes to that member, and the floor travels to one address.

  1. Run the planner

    Put in the full crew size, even the ones who are not sure yet. You can change it in a second.

  2. Copy the message

    The planner writes a short summary with the floor, the total and each share. Paste it in the crew chat.

  3. One member orders

    Press the button under the plan and the order form opens with everything filled in, crew size included.

  4. Roll it out together

    We confirm the total by e-mail, the floor ships by Original Delivery, and the crew has a floor.
