# Mickey's Mailboxes — art direction (written before any code)

Site: emailmickey.com · batch 1128 · builder `site-batch-1128/sites/emailmickey.py`
This folder (`_design/`) is working material; `package.py` skips `_` folders, so it never ships.

## Concept — one sentence

The whole site is ONE DELIVERY ROUND inked on a paper street map: a dashed barn-red route line walks the
visitor from stop to stop, every section stands on its own bit of curb beside a small arch-front mailbox with
the stop number painted on its door, the mailbox flag flips up as you arrive, and every price hangs off its
stop on a manila luggage tag.

## Mood — three words

Neighborly · sturdy · sign-painted.

(Voice: the regular at the hardware counter who has fitted a hundred mailboxes. Plain inches, plain prices,
one dry joke per page at most. US English.)

## Type

Display **Passion One** (400 only: it is already poster-heavy) · body **Roboto Serif** (400 + 600, no italics —
emphasis is done with weight and the theme red, never a slanted face).
Loaded with ONE request: `Passion+One` + `Roboto+Serif:wght@400;600`, `display=swap` (latin ≈ 74 KB total).

Modular scale, ratio **1.25 (major third)**, nine steps, fluid:

measure capped at **66ch**. Size-adjusted local fallbacks (Georgia / Times New Roman at 119 % for the body,
Arial Bold at 77 % for the display) so the swap does not move the layout.

## Spacing scale (4 px base)

`4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 32 · 48 · 64 · 96 · 128` → `--s1 … --s10`.
Stops breathe 64 (phone) to 96/128 (desktop) above and below; inside a stop the steps are 16 / 24 / 32 / 48.

## Grid

Frame `min(1200px, 100% − 2 × gutter)`, gutter 16 (phone) / 24 (tablet) / 32 (desktop). Twelve columns inside the
yard with 24–32 px gaps. The **route lane** is its own column: 96 px wide on desktop, 44 px on a phone. Running
text never exceeds 66ch.

## Icon family — "every icon carries a flag"

Grid 48 × 48, 2.5 px ink stroke, round caps and joins, 3 px corner radii, no fills except ONE small solid
barn-red part per icon (a flag, a tab, an eyelet). Drawn for this trade: wall letterbox, curbside tunnel box,
hopper box, the tall round-shouldered Rocket Pool box, post with arm, number plate, carton, lower-48 pin,
fixings (bolt + anchor), tape measure, key, return arrow. Inline `<symbol>` sprite, `currentColor`.

Product **plans** (dimension drawings) are a second, related family: ink 2 px elevations on paper, steel tint
fill, dimension lines and figures in accent blue — a hardware-counter catalog sheet.

## Signature device — the curbside stop

1. **Route line**: 3 px dashed barn red (10 on / 8 off). It starts at a ring under the header, runs down a
   lane, and on desktop TURNS between stops (rounded elbows, a horizontal run, a street name set on the run:
   "Curbside Ave", "Hopper St", "Tape Measure Ln" …) so the page reads as a route, not a timeline.
2. **Stop marker**: an arch-front mailbox (the shape in the hero photograph) with the stop number on its door
   and a red flag on its side. It stands on …
3. **the curb**: a 3 px ink edge over a 12 px band of concrete with expansion joints every 64 px.
4. **Luggage tags**: manila, clipped corners, a red-ringed eyelet, a twine string, rotated a few degrees;
   prices in Passion One. Tags swing 5° on hover.

Variation down the home page (seven compositions, no two neighbours alike):
A split start (headline + pinned snapshot + four street-sign blades) · B lot plan (six lots divided by dashed
lot lines, icon + tag each) · C snapshot row (three tilted photographs with hanging tags) · D night band
(asphalt, the mini sizer) · E route card (prose + numbered legs + four "we don't" signs) · F ledger (FAQ
accordion + a snow snapshot) · G end of route (a shipping-label contact block and the CTA).

## Header (assigned): two-tier utility bar

Tier 1, ink strip, 13 px: pin + "Grand Rapids, Michigan" · the mailbox address · "Original Delivery across the US".
Tier 2, paper: mailbox-flag mark + wordmark + tagline, nav, CTA "Order a mailbox". Phone: the toggle is a ringed
PLUS that rotates into × and drops a panel. Never three bars.

## Consent banner

A "delivery slip" card (bottom right on desktop, bottom strip on a phone, clear of the hero CTA): Accept all ·
Manage preferences (one switch, off) · privacy link. Ships `hidden`; one localStorage key `mm_cookie`.

## Motion

- Flag flip: each stop's flag starts down and rotates up once (520 ms, cubic-bezier(.34,1.56,.64,1)) when the
  stop is 35 % in view. Without JS the flags are simply up.
- Tags swing on hover (220 ms); buttons press 2 px into their hard shadow; plus → × 200 ms; panel drops 220 ms.
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` → no transitions, no animation, flags up from the start, smooth scroll off.

## Purpose on the first screen (390 and 1366)

Who: houses, duplexes, shared driveways. What: steel mailboxes and lockable parcel boxes with the price on a
tag, shipped across the lower 48. Primary CTA "Order a mailbox". Secondary: "Size your parcel box" (the tool).

## The tool — Parcel Box Sizer (rules, fixed here so page copy, Python and JS agree)

Inputs: biggest usual parcel L × W × H (in), parcels in a typical week (per household), households sharing.
Boxes: wall letterbox (opening 12 × 3, inside run 14, 728 cu in, $59) · parcel drop box (15 × 11, 24,
6,480 cu in, $189) · Rocket Pool (19 × 14, 36, 15,840 cu in, $349).
Rule 1 opening: the two smallest sides must clear the opening. Rule 2 drop: the longest side must fit the
inside run. Rule 3 room: interior ≥ 2 × parcel volume × busy-day parcels × households, busy day = week ÷ 3
rounded up. Pick = the smallest box that passes all three; if only room fails on the Rocket Pool, 2 or 3 of
them; otherwise an honest "nothing we sell takes that". Result hands off to the order form by URL parameters.

## Words this site never uses (gates + brief)

No postal-service names, no character references, no health/finance words; never "stake", "plumb", "repair",
"unlock", "seamless", "journey", "official", "guarantee", "insured", "credit card", "tax", "customs",
"investigate", "kerb" (US: curb), "neighbour" (US: neighbor). "Rocket Pool" never sits next to money words.
