Stop 5 · The shop

How an order runs, from your form to your curb

Mickey’s Mailboxes is a small shop in Grand Rapids, Michigan with one job: putting a decent box at the front of your house. We sell six products, we keep all six on the rack, and we can tell you the inside measurements of each without looking them up.

There is no showroom to drive to and no sales desk. You send the order form, a person at the shop reads it, and the box goes out by ground. If the sizer says a $59 letterbox will do the job, that is the box we will tell you to buy.

What we care about is plain: measurements in inches, prices on tags, fixings in the box and a straight answer about what fits. A mailbox is not a complicated thing. It should arrive whole, go up in an afternoon and still be standing straight when the snow melts.

Some jobs we turn down. We do not come out to fit boxes, we do not engrave names, and we do not ship beyond the lower 48 states. And although the web address has the word in it, we have nothing to do with email: the only mailboxes here are steel.

The five legs of an order

  1. You send the form

    Pick your boxes, a finish and a ship-to state. The total, shipping included, adds up as you go.

  2. We confirm by e-mail

    Before the next working day is out, a person at the bench e-mails your exact total with a link to pay by card. Your card stays untouched until you click it and pay.

  3. We pack at the bench

    The box is checked, the fixings are bagged, the corners are guarded and it goes into a double-wall carton.

  4. Original Delivery

    Ground shipping out of Grand Rapids, off the bench in two working days or less, with its tracking code e-mailed on dispatch.

  5. You fit it

    An afternoon, a drill and a level. The fitting sheet is in the box and we answer fitting questions by e-mail.

Stop 6 · At the counter

Asked at the counter, answered straight

If its two smallest sides are no more than 15 by 11 inches and its longest side is no more than 24, yes. Those are the hopper opening and the height of the locked compartment. A boxed pair of work boots, about 14 by 10 by 5, drops straight in; a boxed office chair does not. The Rocket Pool box opens 19 by 14 and runs 36 inches tall inside. For your own numbers, use the parcel box sizer: it checks the opening, the drop and the room for a busy day.

It is one big box that stands where the carrier already stops: the end of a shared driveway, or between the two doors of a duplex. Any carrier can pull the hopper and drop a parcel in, and the plate behind it keeps hands away from what is already inside. Every household keeps its own key; there are eight in the bag. Whoever gets home first opens the door and takes what is theirs. One household sends the order form with one delivery address, and the rest is between you and the people next door.

Every order ships one way, Original Delivery, by ground out of Grand Rapids in a double-wall carton with corner guards; the carrier's tracking code lands in your inbox on dispatch day. The fixings bag and the fitting sheet ride inside the box. Rates run by region and are set out on the shipping page; the order form adds the right one to your total before you send it.

Most people do, in an afternoon. Dig a hole 24 inches deep and about 8 across, stand the ground sleeve in it, check it with a level on two faces and pack it with concrete or tamped gravel. The post slides into the sleeve and one bolt sets the height. Have your buried lines marked before you dig; every state runs a free locating service. We do not make installation visits, but the fitting sheet is plain and we answer questions by e-mail.

All three colors are the same powder coat, so choose the one you like looking at. What gets a box through a Michigan winter is the layer underneath: the steel is galvanized first and coated second, so a stone thrown by the plow that chips the color still lands on zinc, not bare steel. Rinse the road salt off in spring, and dab any chip with the small pot of matching paint packed in the fixings bag. Iron Black shows salt streaks the most; Lake Blue hides them best.

Return postage is yours, except when the box reached you dented or broken; then it is ours. Your money is back on the same card within five working days of the carton reaching Grand Rapids. Number plates made up with your digits can only come back if we got them wrong. The returns page has the details.
