Stop 1 · Parcel drop boxes

Parcel drop boxes and the Rocket Pool shared box

A parcel on the porch is a parcel in the weather and in plain view until somebody gets home. A drop box gives the carrier somewhere better to leave it: a steel box that takes parcels in at the top and keeps them behind a lock at the bottom.

There are two. The parcel drop box is for one household. The Rocket Pool shared parcel box is the big one, for neighbors who would rather pool a single box than line the driveway with four.

Stop 2 · The hopper

How a parcel gets in, and why it stays in

  1. The carrier pulls the handle

    The hopper tilts out like the drawer of a library book return, and a curved plate behind it swings up to close the chute.

  2. The parcel goes in the hopper

    With the chute shut, the plate stands between a hand and whatever is already inside.

  3. The carrier lets go

    The hopper swings shut under its own weight, the plate drops away and the parcel slides into the compartment below.

  4. You turn the key

    The lower door opens and you lift out your original delivery, as dry and as square as it arrived.

There is nothing to charge, pair or sign in to. Any carrier from any company can use it without being shown how, and a second parcel goes in on top of the first.

The parcel drop box

Parcel drop box

This is the box for one house, or for two that get along. It stands 38 inches tall on a footprint smaller than a kitchen chair, beside the front door or at the top of the drive.

The hopper opening is 15 inches wide and 11 tall, and the locked compartment underneath measures 18 by 15 by 24 inches. That is 6,480 cubic inches: room for half a dozen shoe boxes tumbled in loose, which is how parcels actually land.

The body is 16-gauge galvanized steel, powder coated in any of the three finishes. The lower door opens on a cam lock, and two keys are in the fixings bag. The box has to be anchored, because an empty steel box is something a person can carry: four wedge anchors for concrete and four lag screws for a timber deck are both in the box, so use whichever suits what it stands on.

Stop 4 · For neighbors

The Rocket Pool shared parcel box

Rocket Pool shared parcel box

each$349

Some addresses share more than a fence. A duplex has two doors and one front step; a shared driveway has four houses and one place a carrier can pull in.

The Rocket Pool box is built for that spot. It is a pool because the neighbors pool one box between them, and a rocket because of how it stands: 54 inches tall and round at the shoulders, on a wide foot, like something waiting on a launch pad at the end of the drive.

Inside it has more than twice the room of the drop box, 22 by 20 by 36 inches or 15,840 cubic inches, behind a 19 by 14 inch hopper wide enough for a boxed countertop microwave. Eight keys come in the bag, all cut alike: two each for four households, or however you divide them.

How neighbors use it

  • Stand it where every carrier already stops, on ground one household owns or all of you agree on.
  • Give each home its keys, and put each home’s number plate on the door.
  • Whoever gets home first opens up and takes their own parcels. The rest stay locked in.
  • One household sends the order form and gives one delivery address. We ship it there.

It arrives as two cartons, body and top, and goes together with eight bolts in about a quarter of an hour. It takes two people to stand it up. Then it wants anchoring, and four half-inch wedge anchors are in the box.

Stop 6 · Standing it

Where to put it and how to anchor it

Put the box where the carrier already walks: beside the front door, at the top of the porch steps, or at the mouth of the driveway. A box that is out of the way gets skipped.

It wants a flat, hard base. A concrete pad, pavers on compacted gravel or a timber deck will all do. Check the box with a level before you mark the holes, because a leaning box makes the hopper swing lazy.

Anchoring

On concrete, drill for the wedge anchors with a masonry bit, blow the dust out, tap the anchors in and tighten the nuts. On a deck, drive the lag screws into the joists and not only the boards. Leave the hopper facing the path and a foot of clear ground in front of the lower door so it can swing.

Locks and keys

Each lock has a number stamped on its face. Write it down somewhere other than the fixings bag; a key cutter can make a spare from the number alone. In winter, keep the snow shoveled back from the lower door. The hopper sits high enough to stay clear of most drifts.

Before you drill, four checks.

  • The base is flat and hard, and the box sits level on it.
  • The hopper faces the path the carrier walks.
  • There is a foot of clear ground in front of the lower door.
  • The anchors go into concrete or into deck joists, never into loose pavers alone.

Ready when you are

Both boxes ship by Original Delivery with their anchors in the carton. The order form shows the total, shipping included, before you send anything.
