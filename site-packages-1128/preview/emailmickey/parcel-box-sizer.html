The math, line by line

  1. Biggest parcel: 16 × 12 × 8 in = 1,536 cu in.
  2. Its two smallest sides are 12 × 8 in, so the opening has to be at least that. Its longest side is 16 in, so the run inside has to be at least that.
  3. Busy day: 5 a week ÷ 3 = 1.67, so 2 parcels per household (rounded up).
  4. Room needed: 2 × 1,536 × 2 parcels × 1 household = 6,144 cu in.

Stop 2 · The rules

The three rules it works by

  1. It has to go through the opening

    A carrier feeds a parcel in end first. So the two smallest sides of your biggest parcel have to clear the opening: the middle side against the opening’s width, the smallest side against its height. A 16 by 12 by 8 inch carton presents a 12 by 8 inch end.

  2. It has to drop all the way in

    Once through, the parcel stands or lies on its longest side. That side can be no longer than the longest run inside the box: 14 inches for the wall-mount letterbox, 24 for the parcel drop box, 36 for the Rocket Pool box.

  3. There has to be room on a busy day

    Parcels do not stack like bricks, and they do not arrive one a day. So the sizer wants an interior volume of at least twice the volume of your biggest parcel, for every parcel on a busy day, for every household sharing. A busy day is a third of your typical week, rounded up: five parcels a week means two on a busy day.

The pick is the smallest box that passes all three. If room is the only thing the Rocket Pool box fails on, the sizer says how many of them the sum calls for, up to three. If nothing passes, it says so. We would rather lose a sale than send you a box your parcels bounce off.

Stop 3 · Measuring

How to measure, and what to count

Measure the carton, not what is in it

Length is the longest side, then width, then height. The sizer sorts them for you, so the order you type them in does not matter. Round up to the next quarter inch; cartons bulge.

Size for your biggest regular parcel

That means the one that turns up most months, not the flat-pack bookcase you ordered once. A drop box that takes nine parcels in ten is doing its job. The tenth was always going to need a signature and a strong pair of arms.

Count a typical week

Count parcels per household in an ordinary week, not the week before the holidays. The busy-day rule already allows for deliveries bunching up.

Count every key

If you share, count each household that will hold a key. Two homes in a duplex is two; four on a shared driveway is four. The sizer multiplies the room by that number, which is usually what tips a pick from the parcel drop box to the Rocket Pool box.

Worked example: one house

Biggest parcel 16 by 12 by 8 inches, five a week, one household. The parcel is 1,536 cubic inches. A busy day is two parcels. Room needed: 2 × 1,536 × 2 × 1 = 6,144 cubic inches.

The wall-mount letterbox fails at the opening, because 8 inches will not go through 3. The parcel drop box clears the opening, takes the 16 inch side and holds 6,480 cubic inches. The pick is the parcel drop box, at $189.

Worked example: a duplex

Biggest parcel 14 by 10 by 6 inches, four a week each, two households. The parcel is 840 cubic inches. A busy day is two parcels for each home. Room needed: 2 × 840 × 2 × 2 = 6,720 cubic inches.

The parcel drop box has the opening but only 6,480 cubic inches of room, so it falls just short. The pick is the Rocket Pool shared parcel box, at $349, with a key for each household and six to spare.
