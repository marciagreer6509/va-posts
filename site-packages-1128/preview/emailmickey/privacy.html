Privacy: what we collect and what we do not

Last updated: November 28, 2026

This is the privacy notice for emailmickey.com, the website of Mickey’s Mailboxes, a mailbox shop in Grand Rapids, Michigan. It says what the site learns about you, which is very little, what we do with it, which is answer you and ship your order, and how to make us stop.

What the order form collects, and why

A sent order form hands us your name, the inbox you want replies at, the street the boxes go to, which items and how many, the finish, a house number if you are buying a number plate, and whatever you write in the message. We use them to confirm the order, pack it, ship it and answer your questions. That is the whole purpose.

FormSubmit, an outside service that turns web forms into e-mails, carries the form to us. Your browser posts the form to their server, which forwards it to boxes@emailmickey.com and, because our form asks it to, sends you a short automatic acknowledgment. We do not run a database behind this site. Your order lives in our mailbox and in the paperwork for your shipment.

Payment details never pass through emailmickey.com. The secure payment link in your confirmation is run by a payment processor; the card digits stay with that processor and are never seen at the shop.

The parcel box sizer

The sizer does its sums inside your browser. The measurements you type stay on your own device until you choose to send them over to the order form with the sizer's button, where they travel in the page address as plain numbers such as a length in inches.

Cookies, and the one thing this site stores

On a first visit, a slip at the bottom of the page asks about cookies. Your browser files the answer in its own storage as mm_cookie, so the slip does not keep coming back. Nothing else reads that entry today.

The site sets no analytics cookies and no advertising cookies unless you accept them, and at the time of writing none are installed at all: there is no tracker, no pixel and no tag manager on any page. If that ever changes, this notice changes first and the slip asks again.

The two typefaces are fetched from a public web-font library when a page loads. That request, like any request on the web, shows your IP address to the library’s servers.

Lines the shop does not cross

Mickey's has never sold, rented or traded anyone's details, and never will. Nobody here builds a profile of you for ads. Nobody signs you up for a newsletter; the only e-mails you get from us are about an order or a question you sent.

How long the mailbox holds on

Each order e-mail stays in our inbox for three years, so that we can deal with a return, find a spare key number or answer a question about what you bought, and are then deleted. A question that never turned into a purchase is wiped inside twelve months.

Your rights

Readers in the UK or the EEA are covered by the GDPR. Under it you can request a copy of whatever personal data the shop keeps on you, ask for it to be fixed or erased, ask for it in a format you can take elsewhere, and object to any use we make of it.

Californians have the CCPA. It lets you find out which personal information about you sits in our mailbox, have it erased, and refuse its sale (we sell none), and it bars us from serving you any worse because you asked.

Wherever you live, the way to use them is the same: write to boxes@emailmickey.com from the address you ordered with and say what you want done. A person answers within 30 days, and usually within the week.

Changes to this notice

When the site changes in a way that touches your information, this page is rewritten and the date at the top moves. The version you are reading is the one in force.
