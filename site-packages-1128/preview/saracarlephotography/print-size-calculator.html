The ruleStated in words

The sum behind every answer.

inches = pixels ÷ ppi

Take the number of pixels along one edge of your photo and divide it by the pixels per inch the print needs. Do the same for the other edge. Those two results are the largest sharp print. To turn inches into centimetres, multiply by 2.54.

Worked by hand

4032 ÷ 300 = 13.44 in and 3024 ÷ 300 = 10.08 in

13.44 × 2.54 = 34.1 cm and 10.08 × 2.54 = 25.6 cm

Three distances, three numbers

The further away a print hangs, the fewer pixels per inch the eye can tell apart. The standard rule the calculator uses is this:

In the hand
300 ppi. Prints passed round a table, anything in an album, every page of a photo book.
On a shelf
200 ppi. A frame on a desk, a sideboard or a mantelpiece, seen from an arm’s length or a little more.
Across a room
150 ppi. A frame on the wall or a canvas, seen from a couple of metres.

Megapixels are not a size

Megapixels are simply width × height ÷ 1,000,000. They tell you how much picture there is but not what shape it is, and paper has a shape. Always go by the two edge lengths. The calculator also reports how much each sheet trims from your photo, because an 8 × 10 in sheet is squarer than nearly every camera frame.

Finding the numbersThree places to look

Where to find your photo’s pixel size.

On the phone that took it

Open the photo and swipe up, or tap the details button. The size is listed as two numbers with a multiplication sign between them, such as 4032 × 3024.

On a laptop or desktop

Right-click the file and choose Properties, then Details — or Get Info on a Mac. Look for the line marked Dimensions.

From the megapixels

If all you know is the megapixel count, pick the nearest entry from the list of common sizes in the calculator. It will be within a few pixels of your file.

Too few pixelsWhat to do next

When the numbers say no.

A short file is not the end of the picture. It just wants a different job.

We will not blow a small file up and call it sharp. Software can add pixels, but not detail the camera never recorded, so we would rather print it at the size it can carry.

  • Print it smaller. A 2000 × 1500 pixel photo is soft at A4 but properly sharp as a 6 × 4 in print, or on a page of the Rocket Pool book.
  • Hang it further away. Move the setting from “in the hand” to “across a room” and the same file covers four times the area.
  • Put it on canvas. The weave of a canvas wrap hides fine detail, and nobody reads a canvas at arm’s length.
  • Find the original. A photo that has been through a chat app or lifted from a social site has usually been shrunk. The file on the phone that took it will be several times larger.
