Roll 03Frames, canvas and photo books

Framed prints, canvas wraps and photo books that lie flat.

Four ways to put a photograph where people will see it: behind glazing in an oak, black or white moulding, stretched on canvas, or bound into a book that opens dead flat.

FramesThree mouldings, one plain profile

Framed prints in oak, black and white.

Every frame is made to the size of its print here in the print room: the moulding is cut at 45 degrees, glued and pinned, glazed, backed and fitted with a hanger. It arrives ready for the wall, with nothing to assemble.

Oak

Solid oak with a clear oil finish. Pale, straight-grained and warm, it suits colour photographs and anything with daylight in it.

Black

The same square profile with a satin black finish. It puts a hard edge round a picture, which is why black-and-white prints so often wear it.

White

A satin white finish that lets the wall carry on round the picture. Good for pale, airy photographs and for hanging several in a row.

What is in the A4 frame

Moulding
Plain square profile, 20 mm across the face
Glazing
Clear acrylic, 2 mm — lighter than glass, and it survives the post
The print
A4 on lustre or matte, edge to edge; or A5 inside a bevelled mount
The back
Rigid backing board, taped shut, with a hanger for portrait or landscape
Outside
About 25 × 34 cm

CanvasNo frame, no glazing

Canvas wraps, stretched by hand.

A canvas wrap is a print with nothing in front of it. The picture is printed on canvas, pulled tight over a wooden stretcher and fixed at the back, so it hangs as one clean block of image.

  1. 1

    Printed

    Giclée, in pigment ink, on a 340 gsm cotton-blend canvas with a fine, even weave.

  2. 2

    Sealed

    A satin coat goes over the ink once it has dried, so the surface can be dusted with a dry cloth.

  3. 3

    Stretched

    Pulled tight by hand over kiln-dried pine bars 38 mm deep, with the corners folded neat rather than cut.

  4. 4

    Wedged

    Wooden wedges sit in each corner of the stretcher, so the canvas can be tightened again if it ever slackens.

The edges

We mirror the last few centimetres of the picture round the sides, so the whole photograph stays on the front. If you would rather the picture itself wrapped round, say so in the order notes; you give up about 4 cm from every edge of the front.

The pixels

Canvas is forgiving. The weave hides fine detail and nobody studies a canvas from reading distance, so 150 ppi is plenty. For 16 × 20 in that means a file of 2400 × 3000 pixels or more — the calculator will check yours.

BooksTwenty pages, no gutter

Lay-flat photo books.

In an ordinary book the pages curve into the spine, and whatever sits in the middle of a spread disappears with them. A lay-flat book has no gutter. Each spread is printed as one continuous sheet and mounted back to back with the next on stiff board, so the book opens dead flat and a single photograph can run across both pages.

Sending photos for a book

Number the files in reading order, tell us the title and which picture goes on the cover, and mark any you want across a full spread. We lay the rest out one or two to a page and send you the proof.

We keep our books plain. They are photo books, and wedding albums — leather, presentation boxes and all — are not something we make.

SummerA ready-made layout

The Rocket Pool summer photo book.

Rocket Pool is our ready-made summer layout: a small square lay-flat book with white pages, a turquoise title band and one big picture on every spread. It was drawn for the photographs a holiday by the pool always produces — bright water, hard sun, somebody leaving the poolside like a rocket — and it asks no design decisions of you.

Because the pages are small, the Rocket Pool book is kind to phone photographs: a full page needs only about 1,800 pixels on its long side to print at 300 ppi. It uses the same lay-flat binding as the bigger book, and it travels by Original Delivery, flat between boards.
