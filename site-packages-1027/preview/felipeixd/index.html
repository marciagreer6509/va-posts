Frame 05 · How we workProse · two columns

How a package runs, from first e-mail to hand-over

Felipe IxD is a small interaction-design studio working from Porto, Portugal. It does one kind of work: drawing how software behaves for the people using it. Each package is sold at a fixed price, and the price only covers a scope that has been written down. That is the whole model, and everything else follows from it.

A job starts with the form on the start-a-package page. You pick a package, name the product and the flow, and tell us roughly when you need it. The answer comes back inside two working days as a written scope: which screens, which states, what is out, the delivery date, and the price. If anything in your note is unclear, the reply asks about it before anything else happens.

When you accept the scope, a payment link arrives for the deposit — half the package price — through a secure checkout page. Work begins when the deposit arrives, not before. During the job you see progress at fixed checkpoints, not as a drip of half-finished screens. Annotations are written as we go, so the reasons arrive with the drawings.

Hand-over is a set of files you own, a written summary, and the recorded walk-through. The second half of the price is due then. Two rounds of revisions are included in every package; a third round is quoted separately before it starts, never added silently.

What the studio values

Exactness about states. Most interface trouble lives in the screens nobody drew: the list with nothing in it, the form that fails halfway, the name that is longer than the box. We draw those first, because they are where people give up.

What it turns down

  • Writing the app itself. We do not develop or code anything; your developers build from our files.
  • Hosting, domains, or anything about where the software runs.
  • Marketing, advertising or campaign management of any kind.
  • Promising a conversion rate, a revenue number or any other figure we cannot control.
Frame 06 · QuestionsAccordion · six items

Questions teams ask before booking

What do you need to start an audit?

Access to the flow as a real user would see it — a test account on a staging build is ideal — plus one sentence on what the flow is meant to achieve. If there are known complaints, send them; we treat them as leads to check, not as conclusions. We do not need source code, analytics access or a brief longer than a page.

Do you work with our existing design files?

Yes. If your team already has screens in a shared design file, we work inside a copy of it and keep your layer names and styles where they are sound. For an audit we annotate the shipped product rather than the files, because the files and the product have usually drifted apart.

How long does a prototype take?

Three to four weeks for one user flow, depending on how many screens and edge cases the flow has. The written scope states the exact delivery date before you pay anything. If a flow turns out to be two flows, we say so in the scope and you decide whether to split it.

Who owns the files?

You do, once the final payment is made. That covers the design files, the written fix list or documentation, and the recorded walk-through. We keep the right to show an anonymised still of the work only if you agree to it in writing; by default we show nothing.

Can we pay in two parts?

That is the standard arrangement: half as a deposit when you accept the scope, half on hand-over. Both are paid through a secure link we send by e-mail. Nothing is charged by this website, and nothing is charged before you have agreed to a written scope.

Do you build the app as well?

No. The studio is design only. We hand your developers annotated files, a component sheet or a prototype, and we answer their questions about the design during the hand-over week. Writing the code, hosting it and putting it in front of users stays with your team.
