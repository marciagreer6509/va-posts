Tray 02 · The planner's rules, in words

How the planner decides

No hidden weighting and nothing learned from other shoppers. These ten rules are the whole of it, and the total is plain multiplication of quantities by the list prices.

  1. Nothing to charge: no charger at all. The plan is a lamp and cable kit only.
  2. One phone or one earbud case: one pad.
  3. A watch on the list: the stand, every time. A watch only charges on a raised puck.
  4. Three things to charge: one stand beats three pads. One lead and one footprint.
  5. Phone and earbud case, no watch: the stand on a desk under 100 cm; two pads on anything wider.
  6. Lamp: always one, on the side opposite your writing hand. Back corner, or a third of the way in on desks over 140 cm.
  7. Charger position: front corner on your writing-hand side, so you never reach across the keyboard for the phone.
  8. Cable kits: one for a desk under 100 cm; two for anything wider, a tray either side of your knees.
  9. Mat: included from 100 cm up. Left out below that, because the 80 cm mat would take the lamp's corner.
  10. Speaker: only if you ask for one. It goes at the back, on the side the lamp is not.

Tray 03 · Five steps to a clear desk

Five steps to a clear desk

You can do this in an afternoon with a screwdriver. The order matters: remove what you can first, and only then hide what is left.

1. Empty the desk and count the leads

Take everything off and lay the leads out. Sort them into two piles: things that need power all day (screen, laptop dock) and things that only need topping up (phone, earbuds, watch, lamp, speaker). The second pile is the one you can take off the desk top entirely.

2. Take charging off the leads

Every top-up device with its own cable becomes one pad or one stand. The planner above works out which. This step alone usually removes two or three leads.

3. Swap the plugged-in lamp for a battery one

A lamp lead is the hardest to hide because it starts on the desk top. A rechargeable lamp has no lead to hide.

4. Send the rest underneath

The all-day leads go below the desk top. Fix the cable tray under the back edge, about 5 cm in, with the screws for a wooden top or the adhesive pads for anything you can't drill. Lay the power strip in the tray. Run each lead straight down from its device, through an under-desk clip, and along to the tray. Bundle the slack with a tie and leave a loop about 10 cm long at each device, so you can still slide the screen or the laptop around.

From the tray, one lead runs down a desk leg to the wall. Dress it in the cable sleeve and it reads as part of the leg.

5. Give the surface one mat

A mat under keyboard and pointer marks out the working area, quietens typing, and gives the eye one shape to rest on where there used to be several. Leave the corners of the desk bare for the lamp and the charger.

Cable organiser kit

Everything needed to move a desk's leads from the top to the underside, in one bag. The tray is the main part: a 40 cm powder-coated steel basket that fixes under the back edge and holds a power strip and its adapters off the floor.

Tray 05 · The mat

Felt desk mat

An 80 by 40 cm mat of dense wool-blend felt, 4 mm thick, with a stitched edge and a grippy backing that keeps it where you put it. It is wide enough for a full keyboard and a pointer with room to move, and shallow enough to leave the back of the desk free.

Felt takes the click out of typing and the scrape out of a cup or a phone being set down. A pointer tracks well on it. Crumbs and dust lift off with a lint roller; a mark comes out with a damp cloth and a little patience.

It arrives rolled. Lay it flat under a few books overnight and the curl is gone by morning.
