Wall shelves and bridges

Step shelves and rope bridges: sizes, timber, fixing and prices

These two pieces do most of the work on a cat wall. Shelves are the rungs; a bridge is the long crossing between them. This page gives every measurement, explains what the plywood is and how it meets the wall, and prices both.

Wall step shelf

A$59

The step shelf is a rectangle of 18 mm hoop pine plywood, 40 cm wide and 25 cm deep. That depth is enough for a cat to turn round on and shallow enough that the shelf does not stick out into a hallway. All four corners carry a 3 cm radius and the top edge is rounded over, so there is nothing sharp at head height for the people in the flat either.

The grip grooves are routed rather than glued on, so there is no carpet square to peel or wear through. If you prefer a soft landing, any offcut of rug sits happily on top; the grooves stop it sliding.

Shelves are sold singly because every wall wants a different number. A climb from the floor to a lookout under a standard ceiling takes five; a short hop up to a windowsill takes one or two.

Wall bridge, 90 cm

A$129

The bridge is nine pine planks, each 18 cm wide, threaded onto two runs of 10 mm sisal rope and knotted beneath so the gaps stay even. At either end the rope passes through a plywood ledge 15 cm long that screws to the wall on its own bracket. Ledge to ledge it spans 90 cm.

Span
90 cm overall, including the two end ledges
Walkway
18 cm wide, nine planks with finger-width gaps
Rope
10 mm three-strand sisal, knotted under every plank
Sag
About 6 cm at the middle once hung, by design
In the box
Two brackets fitted to the ledges, eight screws, eight wall plugs, template

A bridge moves. That small sway is the point, and most cats cross it slowly the first week and at a trot after that. Hang it so each end ledge is level with a step shelf, leaving a 10 cm gap between shelf and ledge. The paper template in the box marks both brackets at the right distance so you are not holding a floppy bridge against the wall with one hand.

It needs 90 cm of clear wall at one height, so check for switches, picture rails and door frames before you settle on where it goes.

The timber

Why hoop pine plywood

Hoop pine is a Queensland native grown in plantations a few hours north of Brisbane. As plywood it is pale, close-grained and almost free of knots, which matters when the edge of the sheet is on show. Every piece on this site leaves its edges exposed: you can count the layers, and they take a rounded profile without splintering.

The sheets are 18 mm thick with thirteen thin layers glued crosswise, so a shelf stays flat through a humid Brisbane summer instead of cupping the way a solid board can. The workshop buys full sheets, cuts them on a track saw and rounds every edge on a router table. Offcuts become the end ledges of bridges and the feet of scratching posts; very little goes in the bin.

The finish is a hardwax oil rubbed in by hand and left to dry for two days before packing. It has no gloss and it does not sit on the surface like a varnish, so a scuff can be sanded lightly and re-oiled at home with any furniture oil.

Fixing

How a shelf meets the wall

We do not publish weight figures and we will not tell you a wall is fine without seeing it. What we can do is explain the three situations you are likely to meet and what the box contains for each.

  1. Timber stud behind plasterboard. The usual Queensland interior wall. Studs are upright timbers, commonly 45 or 60 cm apart. Find one with a stud finder or by knocking, and drive the long timber screws through the plasterboard into it. This is the fixing the brackets were designed around.
  2. Brick or block. Drill with a masonry bit to the depth marked on the template, tap in the wall plugs and drive the screws into those.
  3. Plasterboard with no stud where you want the shelf. Move the shelf. The brackets are 24 cm apart, so sliding a shelf a few centimetres left or right nearly always lands one on a stud; the planning page shows how to shift a whole route to suit.

Whatever the wall, mark both bracket holes through the paper template with a spirit level on its top edge, drill, and hang the shelf before moving to the next one. Measure the following rise from the shelf you have just fixed, not from the floor, so small errors do not add up.

Check behind the paint for cables and pipes before drilling, and if the flat is rented, get your landlord's written permission first.
