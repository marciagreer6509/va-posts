View B · a tool you can use now

Tap drill calculator

Marked the hole, about to drill it, and cannot remember the tapping size? Pick the thread.

The rule is the standard one for metric threads: tapping drill = major diameter − pitch. An M6 coarse thread has a 1.0 mm pitch, so the drill is 5.0 mm. The full calculator on the guide sheet also takes any diameter and pitch you type, gives the nearest 0.1 mm drill, and tells you which square and punch suit work of that size.

View C · how the shop runs

A small tool shop, run like a job sheet

UFT Engineering is a small shop in Sheffield that sells one narrow thing: the hand tools used to mark out metal before it is cut. Marking out is the quiet half of model engineering. A casting or a length of bar is blued, stood on a flat plate, and scribed with the lines the drawing calls for. Then every hole is punched where two lines cross. Do that half well and the machining is mostly a matter of working to the lines. Do it badly and no lathe will put the error right.

So the range is short on purpose. Each tool here does a job the others cannot: the plate is the datum, the square carries a line up from it at ninety degrees, the scriber cuts the line, the punches turn a crossing into a start for the drill, the parallels lift the work to a known height and the clamps hold it still. We keep stock of all six on the shelf and we open every box before it goes out.

An order runs through the same few operations every time:

  1. You send the order sheet — the items, quantities and your delivery address. No payment details are asked for on this site.
  2. We check stock and reply — within one working day, by e-mail, with the total including carriage and a secure payment link.
  3. You pay by the link — nothing is charged before that. If you have changed your mind, ignore it and the order lapses.
  4. We check and pack — squares tried against our reference square, points inspected under a glass, steel wiped with oil and wrapped in waxed paper.
  5. The courier collects — within two working days of payment. You receive the tracking reference by e-mail.
  6. You try the tools — the reversal test for the squares is in the guide. Anything not right comes back to us.

What we value is plain: steel that is hard where it should be, faces that are ground and not just polished, and descriptions that say what a tool is and what it is not. We would sooner lose a sale than have a workshop-grade plate bought for a job that needs laboratory paperwork.

Revisions · questions asked before ordering

Six questions, answered straight

What grade is the surface plate?

It is a workshop plate for marking out, not an inspection-room reference. The top face is lapped and we check each one here with a straightedge and feeler gauges before it is packed; it is sold without calibration paperwork. For scribing lines, checking a square and setting a height gauge on a home bench, that is the right plate at the right price. If your work needs a plate with traceable documents, buy from a metrology house instead; we would rather say so than sell you the wrong thing.

How do I check a square is true?

Use the reversal test. Blue a piece of flat plate that has one straight, filed edge. Hold the stock against that edge and scribe a line up the blade. Flip the square over so the stock points the other way, bring the blade back to the foot of the line and scribe again. One line means the square is true. Two lines that part company show twice the error over that length. It takes two minutes, and it is worth doing to any square, ours included, the day it arrives.

How is the granite plate sent?

On its own in a double-walled carton, face up under its cover, with foam corners and a board beneath it. The parcel weighs about nine kilograms and travels by tracked courier, signed for at the door. Smaller tools ordered at the same time go in a second, lighter parcel so they cannot rattle against the stone. Full details are on the carriage and dispatch sheet.

Can I return a tool?

Yes. Unused tools can come back within 30 days of delivery for a refund of the price you paid; you cover the return postage unless the tool was faulty or damaged on arrival, in which case we do. The returns and faulty goods sheet has the steps.

Do you post outside the UK?

No. We send to addresses in England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland only. A granite plate is a poor traveller and we would rather do one country properly.

Do you machine parts to order?

No. UFT Engineering is a tool shop. We do not machine parts, produce drawings or work out anybody's figures, and we do not sell power tools. We sell the hand tools for marking out and include a guide to using them; the making is yours.
