The one mistake to avoid

A person in Malout who turns up on a Tuesday, or a person from the main town who drives to Malout — both are lost patients. Every poster, every WhatsApp post and each Google listing names one place and its timing. Never a poster with no place on it.

Bob's four ideas, judged

KEEP
1. Meta ads with poster-style images
Right. The simple Punjabi poster already works here (329 likes on Malout Live). Use the same style in his own ads, one version per location. Our ads cost far less per person reached than a ₹5,000 page post.
CHANGE
2. WhatsApp group, "add everyone"
The group is fine. Adding people is not. People who get added without asking press "Report", and the number gets banned. It also shows every patient's number to the others — bad for a skin clinic. People must join by their own tap (QR or link).
CHANGE
3. Collect a database, send updates
The WhatsApp members are the database. No Excel list of phone numbers, no bulk SMS. Less work, and no consent problem under India's data law (DPDP Act 2023).
CHANGE
4. Day-parting
Hours of the day do not matter. Days of the week do, and only for Malout: Thursday 6 pm → Sunday 11 am. The main clinic ad simply runs all week on a small daily budget.
MISSING
Google Maps listings, reviews, counting
Not in the draft, and they are free. Someone who types "skin doctor near me" is already looking for him. And without a tally sheet at each reception we will never know what worked, or where.

  2. Create a Community with a name that fits both places, for example "Skin Care Tips – Dr. J.L. Bansal". Use only its Announcements group.

  4. Set a greeting message (automatic reply to a first chat): both places with timings and map pins, then the invite link with "Join for reminders and skin tips".

Three WhatsApp rules

  • Never add a number yourself. People join by scanning or tapping. At reception, ask: "Shall we send you reminders?" and let them scan.
  • No medical advice in chat. No photos of skin problems requested or diagnosed over WhatsApp. The answer is always "come and see the doctor", with the right place.
  • No patient details in the group. No names, no photos, no "see this case".

The rules box

Two rule-books apply: the Indian doctors' ethics code, and Meta's health-ad rules. The same do/don't list satisfies both.

Do

  • Announce facts: name, degree, registration number, what he treats, day, time, place, phone.
  • Share health education: tips, "see a doctor early", seasonal care.
  • Announce changes: "not sitting in Malout this Sunday", "clinic closed on…", "back from…".
  • Say a service is available (laser, PRP).
  • Use plain graphics, or a neutral photo of the building or signboard.
  • Ask for Google reviews in person, with no reward.

Don't

  • No "best", "No. 1", "most experienced", "famous".
  • No "guaranteed", "100% cure", "permanent", "in 7 days". Above all for white patches, psoriasis and baldness.
  • No patient testimonials or patient photos in ads. Do not paste Google reviews into posters.
  • No before/after pictures. Meta rejects them too.
  • No "Do you have pimples?" / "Losing your hair?" Meta bans ads that speak to the viewer's own condition. Say "Treatment for…" or "Skin care in winter".
  • No close-up photos of diseased skin.
  • No discounts, "free check-up" offers or price comparisons.
  • No commission to chemists or anyone for sending patients. Cards only.

Where the law stands, honestly

The 2023 NMC conduct rules were put on hold on 23 August 2023, and the older 2002 ethics code is back in force. Its chapter 6 says a doctor must not solicit patients, and lists what he may announce. Read strictly, any paid ad sits in a grey zone — including the poster he already pays for. In practice, plain "doctor, day, time, place" posters are everywhere and health education is expressly allowed. Our plan stays at that safe end. But it is his registration, so show him this box and let him decide.

How to measure — per location

One tally sheet at each reception

Print the location name on top. For every new patient, ask one question and make one mark:

ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ ਡਾਕਟਰ ਸਾਹਿਬ ਬਾਰੇ ਕਿੱਥੋਂ ਪਤਾ ਲੱਗਾ?

"Where did you hear about the doctor?"

Columns: Facebook / Instagram · WhatsApp · Google · Malout Live · Chemist or another doctor · Family or friend · Board / card.

Malout: one sheet per Sunday. Main clinic: one sheet per week.

Four numbers a week, for each location

  • New patients (and total patients).
  • The tally by source.
  • That campaign's spend and its chats ("Clinic timing?" vs "Malout Sunday timing?").
  • Reviews on that location's Google listing.

Before starting: count one normal week at the clinic and two normal Sundays in Malout. That is the baseline.

After 8 weeks: move the held-back ₹2,500, and if needed part of the ad money, to the location that responds.

Skip these

  • A website. The two Google listings do the job for free.
  • Daily posting, reels, YouTube. Nobody has the time.
  • Malout ads from Monday to Wednesday. People forget by Sunday.
  • Hour-by-hour day-parting. No gain at this size.
  • One poster listing both places inside an ad. It halves the clarity. One ad, one place.
  • Two WhatsApp numbers or two groups. Double work for the VA.
  • Interest targeting, lookalikes, many ad sets. One town, one ad set.
  • Bought phone lists, bulk SMS, bulk WhatsApp tools. Bans and consent trouble.
  • Google search ads. Too few searches in small towns. The free listings catch them.
  • Fake or paid reviews. Never.

First week checklist

  1. Get four facts from the doctor: the main clinic's address, its days and timings, its phone, and where the laser and PRP are done. Also ask whether the main clinic is already busy.
  2. Start both tally sheets and count the baseline.
  3. Search Google Maps for both places and for the doctor. Then create or claim one listing per location. Google usually asks for a short video check — record each one on the spot with the signboard in view.
  4. Set up WhatsApp Business, the Community, the QR.
  5. Print the QR stands, boards and cards for both places.
  6. Make the Facebook Page, the posters in two versions, the two campaigns. Start the Malout one on a Thursday.
