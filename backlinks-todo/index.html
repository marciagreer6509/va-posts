Once you know the pattern, however, most of them turn out to be familiar vitamins wearing their formal names.

Take the ingredient list of VL Plus Spray as a worked example for readers in Ireland. Vitamin C is listed as L-ascorbic acid and sodium ascorbate, two forms of the same vitamin. Zinc is added as zinc gluconate. Vitamin B6 appears as pyridoxine HCl, and vitamin B12 as methylcobalamin. Niacin, pantothenic acid, riboflavin and thiamine are simply the names of vitamins B3, B5, B2 and B1, and biotin is biotin.

Order matters as well. Further down you will see natural blackberry flavour, potassium sorbate and a thickener called xanthan gum, all of which have an everyday job: taste, keeping the liquid stable and giving it body.

The table of nutrients per daily dose adds a second layer. The B vitamins and biotin reach the full nutrient reference value, zinc covers a large share of it and vitamin C about half. L-citrulline, L-glutamine and L-carnitine have no reference value, so they carry a dash instead of a percentage. Four authorised health claims apply to the vitamins and zinc, covering tiredness and fatigue, energy-yielding metabolism, the immune system and hair.

A tidy label does not make a supplement suitable for everyone.
