What we need from you

A grade can only be as good as the material it starts from, and the most common delay in any grading suite is a missing file. So before we quote, we ask for the following, and we check it the day it lands.

  • The locked edit as EDL or XMLFrom your editing timeline, exported with the reel names or file names intact so every event points back to a real camera file.
  • The camera original mediaThe files the camera wrote, not transcodes, so we grade from every bit of latitude the sensor recorded. Proxies are fine for the edit; they are not for the grade.
  • A reference export with burnt-in timecodeWe match the conform against it frame by frame. It is also how we catch a speed change, a reframe or a title the edit list does not describe.
  • Camera and colour spaceWhich camera, which log curve or gamut it recorded in, and whether any look was baked in on set.
  • The delivery sheetWhere the film is going and in what format, so we master once and correctly.

If any of this is missing we tell you what and why before the schedule starts, never after.

Parade · primaries

How a grade runs, pass by pass

Balance. Each shot is brought to a neutral, consistent starting point with primary corrections: exposure set on the waveform, black level placed so shadows hold detail without going milky, white balance taken from something that should read as white. On a log source this is also where the transform into the working colour space happens.

Match. Shots within a scene are matched to each other so a reverse angle does not jump in warmth and a pickup shot on a different day sits inside the scene. The vectorscope is the arbiter here; skin tones from different setups are brought onto the same line.

Secondaries. With the base balanced, we isolate what needs its own treatment: a face under a mixed practical, a sky that clipped, a wardrobe colour that fights the production design, a sign that pulls the eye. Windows and keys are tracked so they hold through camera moves.

Look. The look sits on top of the balanced base — the contrast curve, the colour of the shadows and highlights, the density of the image. If you came through look development, this is where the agreed look is applied and refined shot by shot. If not, we propose one from your references in the first pass.

Watch-down. The whole cut is played in sequence, at speed, on a calibrated Rec.709 display. A grade that holds shot by shot can still drift across a scene; this pass is where it is caught.

Notes and sign-off. You receive a review file of the first pass. Two rounds of notes are included; each comes back as a new review file. When you sign off, the grade is locked and the masters are rendered.

Music videos

Music videos are graded as one piece at a flat €320 for up to four minutes. They carry their own demands: strobing light, coloured haze, practicals in shot, fast cutting that leaves no room for a shot that does not match. We grade them for the screen they will mostly be watched on and for the platform's compression, keeping saturated stage colour from blocking up into flat patches once it is squeezed.

A cut longer than four minutes is priced at the per-minute rate instead. Lyric overlays and graphics are graded only where they sit over picture you supply as part of the timeline.

Brand films

Brand films are graded at the per-minute rate. They often need a product colour held exactly across lighting setups; send us the reference colour as a physical sample photographed under daylight, or as a value from the brand guide, and we hold it on the vectorscope through every shot it appears in.
