How a grade runs at Mediacroma

Mediacroma is a small grading studio in Braga that takes finished edits and makes the picture whole. We do not shoot and we do not cut; we arrive after both, which is the point. By the time a film reaches us its story is settled, and the remaining question is how it should look and feel in every frame.

A job starts with your booking: the running time, the formats you need and any references. We reply with a written quote and a schedule. Once the deposit is paid we conform the edit against the camera originals and check it against your reference export, cut by cut, before a single colour decision is made.

Then the grade itself. Primaries first, setting exposure, black level and white balance so each shot sits on the waveform where it should. Secondaries next: skin isolated and held on the vectorscope's skin line, skies and walls separated, a distracting colour in the background pulled down. Then the look, applied over a balanced base rather than used to hide an unbalanced one, and a final pass watching the cut in sequence for continuity of light.

You receive a first pass as a review file. You send notes with timecode; we work through them, in a live remote session if you prefer to be in the conversation. Two rounds of notes are part of every grade. When you sign off we render the masters to your specification, check them against the delivery sheet and send them by transfer link.

What we value is a grade that serves the film rather than announcing itself. Skin that looks like skin under the light it was lit with. Night that stays readable. Contrast decided by the story, not by a preset.

What we turn down is just as plain. We take locked edits only, because grading a cut that is still changing wastes your money. We have no cameras for hire and no crew. We do not edit from raw footage, mix or master sound, or place your film as advertising. If your project needs any of those, we will say so at the first reply rather than after the deposit.

Questions producers ask before booking

If yours is not here, write to us with the running time and the formats you need, and we will answer it with a quote.

What should I send?

A locked edit as an EDL or XML, the camera original files the edit points to, and a reference export of the cut with burnt-in timecode so we can check the conform against it. Add any references for the look: frames from the shoot, stills you like, a note on what the light should feel like. If the film was shot log, tell us the camera and the colour space it recorded in.

How long does a grade take?

It depends on the length of the cut and how many looks it holds. Before you pay the deposit we send a written schedule with the date the first pass reaches you and the date the masters are due. A music video is usually a matter of days rather than weeks; a longer short film takes more.

Do you supply LUTs?

Yes, when they are useful. Look development ends with test LUTs for on-set monitoring, and on request we export a viewing LUT of the finished grade for your editor's dailies or trailer work. A LUT is a monitoring aid; the finished grade itself is delivered as rendered masters.

Can I attend the grade?

Yes, remotely. A review session is an hour on a live, colour-managed stream of the suite at €60 per hour, and we go through your notes together on the same frame. We do not run in-person attended sessions.

Which formats do you deliver?

A Rec.709 SDR master as standard, in the codec and wrapper your distributor or festival asks for, plus compressed review files. An HDR master is the €120 add-on. Send us the delivery specification and we work to it line by line.

What if I want changes?

Two rounds of written notes are included in every grade. Notes are best given as timecode plus what you see and what you want instead, for example warmer skin, deeper blacks, a cooler window. Further rounds are booked as review sessions.
