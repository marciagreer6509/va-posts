Conform · relink

The conform, from your edit list

Editing happens on lightweight proxies; grading has to happen on the camera originals. The conform is the bridge. We take the EDL or XML from your editing timeline and rebuild every event in the grading timeline against the original media, at full resolution and full bit depth.

Then we check it. The conformed timeline is laid against your reference export and compared cut by cut. We look for the things edit lists describe badly: speed ramps, reframes and repositions, flipped shots, nested sequences, dissolves of non-standard length, stabilised shots, temporary titles. Each one is either rebuilt to match or written into a short list for you to answer.

You get that list before grading starts. A conform that is quietly wrong produces a beautiful grade of the wrong frames, and nobody notices until the film is screened.

  • ExportAn EDL or XML from the locked timeline, with file names or reel names and source timecode preserved.
  • RelinkEvery event pointed at the camera original, at its native resolution and colour space.
  • CompareThe rebuilt timeline checked against your burnt-in reference, frame by frame at every cut.
  • ReportA short written list of anything that did not match and how it was resolved.
Gamut · HDR / SDR

HDR and SDR deliverables

Every grade is delivered as a Rec.709 SDR master. It is the format most screens and most festivals still expect, and it is the version most people will see. We grade on a calibrated Rec.709 display so what you approve is what you receive.

An HDR deliverable is a second master made for high dynamic range displays, with brighter highlights and a wider colour gamut. It is not a conversion of the SDR master. Each shot is revisited: how far a practical lamp or a sunlit window should glow, how much the extra range is allowed to pull the eye away from a face, how deep blacks can go before shadow detail is lost on a bright living-room screen. Some films use the extra range boldly; most are better served by restraint.

Because HDR changes the way the picture reads, we recommend that you review the HDR master in a remote session where possible, or on a reference-grade display on your side. Consumer HDR displays vary widely and a phone screen at full brightness is not a fair judge.

The HDR add-on is €120 per project on top of the grade. It includes the HDR master and the metadata your delivery sheet asks for. It does not include re-grading after the SDR master is signed off; changes to the base grade are made first, then carried into both masters together.

Delivery · formats

Formats we deliver

We work from your delivery sheet. Send us the specification from the festival, the distributor or the client, and we master to it line by line: resolution, frame rate, codec, wrapper, colour space and transfer, audio layout if you send us a finished mix to lay back, and the file naming they require.

Without a specification we deliver a mezzanine master in a high-quality intra-frame codec, a Rec.709 file for the web compressed with care for skin and gradients, and a review file. We keep the graded project archived so that a late request for another format does not mean repeating the grade.

Audio is your mix, laid back as delivered. We do not mix, balance or master sound.
