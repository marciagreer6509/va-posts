The air-dry clay kit

The air-dry clay kit, 4 makers, costs $36. Air-dry clay hardens on its own at room temperature, so there is no kiln and no oven anywhere in the process. Make a pinch pot, a trinket dish, a set of beads or a small animal, leave it on a shelf, then paint it.

  • Four sealed packs of white air-dry clay, one per maker, enough for two or three small pieces each.
  • A set of wooden shaping tools: a cutter, a smoother, a loop tool and a pointed scribe for drawing into the surface.
  • Six paints in pots with four brushes, for decorating once the clay is fully dry.
  • A small clear varnish to give painted pieces a soft sheen.
  • The picture guide, with pinch pots, coils, slab dishes and how to join two pieces so they stay joined.

Drying time

Thin pieces dry in about a day; thick ones can take three. The guide shows how to tell: the clay turns from cool gray-white to a lighter, chalky white and no longer feels cold. Paint too early and the color cracks. Turn pieces over halfway through drying so the bottom dries too.

Clay mess notes

Wet clay smears; dry clay crumbles. Work on a placemat or a sheet of baking paper, keep a damp cloth nearby for hands, and let the crumbs dry before sweeping them up rather than wiping them into a paste. Opened clay dries out within a day, so press any leftover tightly back into its pouch.

The paper marbling kit

The paper marbling kit costs $29. Marbling floats color on top of a thickened bath, then lifts the pattern onto paper in one movement. Each sheet is a one-off.

  • Marbling size powder, which you whisk into water the night before so the bath is thick enough for color to float.
  • Six marbling colors with droppers for each.
  • A fold-flat tray, a wide-tooth comb and a pointed stylus for dragging patterns.
  • Pre-treated paper, cut to fit the tray: twenty-four sheets.
  • The picture guide, with stone, wave, feather and nonpareil patterns drawn step by step.

How a marbling session runs

Mix the size and let it rest overnight. Next day, pour it into the tray, drop the colors on, comb or swirl, lay a sheet down, lift it off and rinse it gently. Once the bath is ready each sheet takes a minute or two, and an hour gives a family a stack of patterned paper for card covers, bookmarks and gift wrap. Sheets dry flat in a few hours.

Marbling mess notes

This is a wet craft. Put the tray on an old towel, keep a second tray or a sink for rinsing, and lay newspaper where sheets will dry. The colors wash off skin with soap; they will stain fabric.

Ages for both kits

Both kits are for ages six and up with an adult. Clay suits the youngest makers best of all our crafts. With marbling, the adult mixes the size and handles the full tray; makers drop colors and lift sheets.
