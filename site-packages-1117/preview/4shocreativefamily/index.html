How a kit gets from our table to yours

4Sho Creative Family is a small kit studio in Atlanta, Georgia. We choose the materials, test every step at a real kitchen table, write and draw the picture guides, and pack each box by hand. What we sell is the box itself: the loom, the blocks, the clay, the trays and colors, and pages that show what to do with them.

Here is how an order runs. You choose a craft on the order page and hit send. Back comes an email with the kit price plus shipping and a secure link for paying it — no money moves unless you follow that link. When the payment shows up, your box is packed and handed to the carrier within two working days, and a tracking link follows by email. When it arrives, the picture guide takes over.

What we care about is a first session that works. That means warp cotton already cut to length, clay that stays soft in its pouch until you open it, inks that wash off with soap, and guides that show each step as a drawing first and a sentence second, so a six-year-old can follow along while the adult reads.

  1. Pick a kit. Choose a craft, how many boxes, and the region it ships to. The order form adds it up as you go.
  2. Check your email. Our reply carries the sum and a secure pay link. Pay whenever suits you.
  3. We pack and post. Within two working days of payment, with a tracking link sent the moment it leaves.
  4. Lay it out and make. Start with the set-up page in the picture guide, then work through the steps together.

There are things we leave to others. We do not run classes or workshops, and we do not offer childcare of any kind: the kits are made for a family at home, with an adult at the table. We do not make kits for children under six, because the tools and small parts are not right for them. And we do not build custom or one-off kits; every box is one of the six on this page, packed the same careful way each time.

Questions families ask before they order

What age is each kit for?

Every kit is made for makers aged six and up working beside an adult. Weaving and marbling suit six-year-olds well once the grown-up has set up the loom or the tray. Block printing needs the most adult hands: the carving is done by, or right next to, a grown-up, and younger makers do the inking and pressing.

How long does a kit take?

Plan on one long afternoon or two shorter sittings. Warping a frame loom takes around twenty minutes, and the weaving itself spreads over a couple of evenings. A block print session runs about ninety minutes from first sketch to the first pull. Clay is quick to shape but needs a day or more on a shelf to dry before painting. Marbling is the fastest: once the size has rested, each sheet takes a minute or two.

How messy is it?

Honestly, it depends on the craft. Weaving leaves nothing but yarn snips. Clay leaves crumbs that sweep up once dry. Block ink and marbling color are water-based and wash off hands and most tables with warm soapy water, but they can mark clothes, so cover the table and wear old shirts. Every picture guide starts with a set-up page that shows how we lay out the table.

Can I buy refills?

Yes. The refill pack ($14) restocks the materials that get used up in one kit: yarn for weaving, inks and paper for printing, clay and paint for the clay kit, or colors and paper for marbling. Pick which kit it is for on the order form.

How fast do you ship?

Kits leave our Atlanta table within two working days of payment. Delivery then takes two to three working days in Georgia and the neighboring Southeast states and three to six working days elsewhere in the lower 48. The shipping page has the costs and the Alaska and Hawaii times.

Can I return an opened kit?

A kit whose inner pouches are still sealed can come back within 30 days. Once clay, ink or marbling size has been opened we cannot resell it, so we do not take those back — but if anything arrives damaged or missing, opened or not, write to us and we will send the part or refund it.
