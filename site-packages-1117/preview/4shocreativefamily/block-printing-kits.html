What is in the block printing box

The block printing kit, 4 makers, costs $44. It is our busiest kit, the one where everyone has a job: one person carves, one rolls the ink, one lines up the paper, one lifts the print.

  • Four lino-style soft blocks, a rubbery carving surface that cuts like firm cheese and needs no heating.
  • Two gouge handles with a V-shaped tip for fine lines and a U-shaped tip for clearing larger areas, each with a cap.
  • One brayer, the small rubber roller that spreads ink thin and even across the block.
  • Three water-based block inks: deep blue, warm orange and black, in squeeze tubes.
  • An inking tray to roll the ink out on, tracing paper and a soft pencil for transferring a drawing.
  • Forty sheets of smooth paper in two sizes, plus four blank cards with envelopes.
  • The picture guide, with drawn steps for sketching, transferring, carving safely, inking and pulling a print.

Carving, with an adult beside you

Soft blocks are far kinder than old-style linoleum, but a gouge is still a cutting tool. The guide is clear on this: an adult carves, or sits right beside the maker who is carving, every time. We draw the safe grip on the first page of the carving section — the hand that is not holding the gouge stays behind the blade, and the block is turned rather than the tool. Makers six to eight usually draw the design and let the grown-up cut it; from around nine, many carve their own simple shapes with an adult watching.

Anything you carve away stays white on the print. Anything you leave raised picks up ink. The guide starts with a leaf and a star so the reversal makes sense before anyone tries letters, which print backward unless you carve them backward.

Inking and pressing

Squeeze a short line of ink onto the tray and roll the brayer back and forth until it makes a soft hiss and looks like velvet. Roll it over the block in two directions. Lay the paper on top, rub with the back of a spoon or the flat of a hand, and peel it off from one corner. That first peel is the moment everyone crowds round to see. Every block will print dozens of times; rinse it under the tap to change color.

How long it takes

A first session runs about ninety minutes: twenty for drawing and transferring, half an hour or more for carving, and the rest for printing and changing colors. Prints are dry to the touch within an hour and ready to stack the next day. Cards made on the first evening can be in the mail by the weekend.

Mess notes

The inks are water-based and wash off hands, the brayer and a wipeable table with warm soapy water. They will mark fabric, so cover the table with old newspaper or a cheap plastic cloth, and roll sleeves up. Carving makes little curls of soft block that sweep straight into the trash.

Refills for block printing

The blocks, gouges and brayer last. A refill pack ($14) for block printing brings fresh tubes of all three inks and another forty sheets of paper. Blank blocks are not in the refill; carve the back of an existing block for a new design.

What this kit is not

It is a hand-carved block kit for home tables, not a press and not fabric dye. The inks are meant for paper and card. We do not sell carved blocks with designs already cut, and we do not take custom design requests.
