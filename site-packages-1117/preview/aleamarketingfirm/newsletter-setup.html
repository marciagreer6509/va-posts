The template

We design a single reusable newsletter layout for your shop. It starts from what you already have: the colors on your sign, the type on your shopping bags, the feel of your front window. From there we build a header with your shop name, a lead story block, a row for two or three featured items, a short note block for news or events, and a footer carrying your opening hours, your shop address as you choose to show it, and a clear unsubscribe link.

The template is built inside your own sending account, tested on narrow phone screens first and on wide desktop inboxes second, because that is the order most shop customers read in. We check it in dark mode, check that the images carry alt text, and check that the main button is big enough to tap with a thumb.

The sign-up form

A list grows from the sign-up form, so we write and style one for your website and, if you like, a short version for a tablet at the till. It asks only for what you will actually use, usually an email address and a first name, and it turns on double opt-in: a new subscriber confirms by clicking a link before they are added. That single step keeps typos, fake addresses and accidental sign-ups out of your list from day one.

If you run a website builder that has its own form block, we connect that instead and match the wording. We will tell you plainly which form we used and where it sits.

The welcome email

The welcome email is the most-opened message a shop ever sends, because it arrives the moment somebody has said yes. We write one that says who you are, what the newsletter will bring and how often, and gives the new subscriber one good reason to come into the shop or visit your site. It goes out automatically to everyone who confirms.

Face two

What we need from you

Setup moves quickly when the material arrives in one go. Before we start, we ask for:

  • Access to your sending account, or a short call by email about which one to open if you do not have one yet.
  • Your logo or shop sign as a file, and any colors or typefaces you already use.
  • Six to ten photos of the shop, the shelves and the things you sell. Phone photos are fine; we crop and tune them.
  • A few lines in your own words about the shop: what it sells, who comes in, what you would like people to know.
  • Access to your website editor, or the name of whoever looks after it, so the sign-up form can go in.

Nothing on that list needs to be polished. Half the job is turning rough notes and phone pictures into something that reads well in an inbox.

Face three

How the setup weeks run

Once your booking is confirmed and the deposit paid, we send a short list of questions and a link for uploading photos. Within about a week you receive a first look at the template with sample copy written for your shop, so you can judge it the way a subscriber will.

You get two rounds of changes on the template. Most shops ask for small things in the first round, a different photo, a warmer color, a shorter header, and approve in the second. We then put the sign-up form live, write the welcome email, send a test of it to you, and switch it on when you are happy.

At the end you receive a short handover note listing where everything lives in your account, how the form is connected and how to edit the welcome email yourself if you ever want to. If you go on to a monthly plan, the first issue usually follows within the next two weeks.

Setup on its own is fine too. Some shops prefer to write their own issues once the template exists, and the setup is built so that they can.
