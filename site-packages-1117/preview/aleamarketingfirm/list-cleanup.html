List clean-up

One-off, $180. A tidier subscriber list: bounces removed, consent checked, and one friendly re-engagement send to the people who have gone quiet.

Face one

Why a list needs tidying

Lists drift. Some addresses stop working when people change jobs or close old inboxes. Some people signed up years ago at the till and have not opened anything since. Some were added from a spreadsheet nobody remembers making. Every one of them still counts against the size of your list, and sending to addresses that bounce or never open slowly teaches inbox providers to treat your newsletter as unwelcome.

A clean-up resets that. It is a one-off job for $180, done inside your own sending account, and it is the right first step for any shop with a list that has gone untouched for a while.

Face two

The three passes

Removing bounces

First we take out the addresses that cannot receive mail at all: hard bounces, addresses with obvious typos in the domain part, duplicates and role addresses that nobody reads. Where a typo is clear, a missing letter in a common inbox domain for example, we list it for you rather than guessing, so you can decide whether to ask that customer at the counter.

Checking consent

Next we look at where each group of subscribers came from. People who joined through a sign-up form, ticked a box at checkout or confirmed by double opt-in stay. Addresses imported from somewhere with no record of permission are set aside in a separate segment and are not mailed again unless you can tell us how they agreed. We explain each group in plain terms; the decision stays yours.

The re-engagement send

Finally we write one short, friendly email to subscribers who have not opened or clicked in a long time. It says, in your shop's voice, that you would love to keep writing to them and asks them to click once if they would like to stay. Those who click stay on the list. Those who do not are moved to an inactive segment after two weeks, not deleted, so nothing is lost if you change your mind.

Face three

What you get back

At the end you receive a short summary by email: how many addresses were removed and why, how many were set aside for missing consent, how many answered the re-engagement send, and how large your active list is now. The cleaned list stays exactly where it was, in your own account, tidied and segmented. We never keep a copy.

The clean-up also includes a quick look at your sign-up form. If it is missing double opt-in or asks for more than you need, we say so and, if you like, switch double opt-in on so the list stays clean afterwards.

A smaller list after a clean-up is normal and a good sign. A list of people who open is worth far more to a shop than a large one that sits silent, and every issue after that goes to readers who want it.

Many shops book the clean-up together with setup, or just before starting a monthly tier, so the first proper issue reaches a list in good order. You can combine them on the booking form and the total is shown as you choose.
