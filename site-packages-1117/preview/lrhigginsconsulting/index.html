2Step 2 · What we write

Documents for the way your place actually runs

A small business usually runs on habits nobody wrote down: the order the lights go on, the drawer that sticks, the call that has to happen before ten. We turn those habits into steps.

Owner interviews

Every document starts with you talking through the work while we take notes. We ask what happens first, what goes wrong, and what you check before you leave for the night.

Full operations manuals

One bound reference for the whole business: daily routines, recurring tasks, who holds which key, which supplier to call, and where every spare part lives.

Opening, closing and weekly checklists

Short lists that sit by the door or the register. Each line is a single action a person can tick, in the order the room actually needs it done.

Task procedures with photos

Numbered steps for the jobs that are easy to get slightly wrong, with a photograph at the point where most people hesitate.

New-starter packs

What a new team member reads, sees and does in the first week, so the owner is not repeating the same tour every time someone joins.

A yearly refresh

Routines drift. Once a year we go back through a manual with you, rewrite the steps that changed and issue a new version with a change log.

?Step 4 · How a job runs

From a conversation to a manual on the shelf

We are a small writing practice in Raleigh, North Carolina. The work is narrow on purpose: we write down how a business does its everyday jobs, and we do it in the owner’s own vocabulary so the pages sound like the place they describe.

A job begins when you send the request form. We reply by email to agree which documents you need and confirm the fixed price, then a deposit link follows in the same thread. Your card stays untouched until you have read that reply and agreed to it.

Next comes the owner interview. For a checklist set that might be one hour; for a full operations manual it is usually three shorter sessions, because people remember more when they are standing in the room at the time the task happens. We record the order of actions, the tools and supplies involved, the points where a mistake is costly, and the small judgment calls an experienced team member makes without thinking.

From those notes we write a first draft and send it to you. You mark anything that is wrong or missing, ideally with the person who does the task each day. We revise, you check again, and the final files arrive in an editable format with a version number on every page.

What we value is plainness. One action per line, verbs first, no jargon you would not use yourself. What we turn down is anything that is not writing about your routines: we keep away from accounting and tax, legal and HR matters, hiring and staffing, and we do not set up or configure software for you.

5Step 5 · Questions owners ask

Questions owners ask before they book

How long does a manual take?

A full operations manual usually runs four to six weeks from the first owner interview to the final files, because most of the time is spent waiting for you to check drafts between shifts. A checklist set is normally finished within two weeks, and a new-starter pack within three.

Do you visit the business?

Inside Raleigh and the nearby Triangle towns we can come in, watch the opening or closing routine and take the step photos ourselves, at no extra charge. Farther away, the interviews happen by video call and you or a team member send the photos from a short shot list we write for you.

What format do we get?

Every document arrives as an editable word-processing file and as a print-ready PDF. Checklists are laid out to print on a single letter-size sheet each, and the manual comes with a numbered contents page and a version line on every page.

Can staff edit it?

Yes, and they should. The files are yours, unlocked, with plain headings and no special fonts, so a shift lead can change a step on the day it changes. We add a short page explaining how to record an edit so the version history stays readable.

What does a refresh cover?

The yearly refresh ($480) is a one-hour interview about what changed, a full read-through of the existing manual, rewritten steps and checklists where the work has moved on, new photos where you supply them, and a fresh version number with a dated change log.

Do you give hiring advice?

No. A new-starter pack describes how your business welcomes and walks a new person through their first week. Who to take on, what to pay and what goes into a contract are decisions for you and your own advisers, and we do not write about them.
