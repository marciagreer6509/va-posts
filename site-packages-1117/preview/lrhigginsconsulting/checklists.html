1Step 1 · Why a checklist

A checklist is not a manual, and that is the point

A manual explains. A checklist reminds. The person locking up at the end of a long shift does not need the reasons behind each step; they need a list short enough to read standing up, in the order they will walk around the room. That is what we write.

We keep each checklist to one letter-size page. Every line starts with a verb and names one action that can be ticked: switch off the display lights, count the float into the safe bag, check the back door bolt from the inside. If a line needs explaining, the explanation goes into a task procedure and the checklist points to it by number.

A good checklist also leaves things out. If everyone already does a step without fail, putting it on the list only makes the list longer and the important lines easier to skip. Part of the owner interview is deciding what deserves a line.

2Step 2 · The three kinds

Opening, closing and weekly

The opening routine

Written in walking order from the door you come in by: alarm, lights, heat or cooling, the first checks on stock and cash, what has to be ready before the first customer. We note anything that must happen before a set time, such as accepting an early delivery.

The closing routine

The list that matters most, because mistakes at closing are found the next morning. Cash, doors, windows, appliances, the things that must be switched off and the things that must be left on. We end every closing list with a final walk-round line and a space for initials.

The weekly list

Jobs that come round once a week and are easy to let slide: the deep clean of one area, the stock check, the supplier order, the noticeboard update. Each line carries a day of the week so the list can be split across shifts.

?Step 3 · A decision

Do you need checklists or procedures?

Can a trained person do it from a one-line reminder?
Yes it belongs on a checklistNo it needs a task procedure

Most businesses need both. The checklist is the daily tool; the procedure is the reference a new person reads the first few times. When we interview you for a checklist set, we flag any line that is really a procedure in disguise and tell you, so you can decide whether to add a procedure set later. We never add documents to a job without asking.

5Step 5 · What we leave out

Things our checklists do not cover

We write down how your business does its own work. We do not write compliance checklists for inspections, safety audits or anything an outside body might rely on, and we do not decide what rules apply to you. If a checklist line comes from a rule you already follow, we write it in your words, exactly as you describe it.

  • Accounting or tax routines beyond the steps you already run
  • Legal or HR wording of any kind
  • Software set-up or configuration
