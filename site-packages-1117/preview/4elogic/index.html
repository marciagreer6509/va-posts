What leaves the bench

Interlocking, dissecting, sliding and hiding

Six- and twelve-piece burrs

Notched square sticks that lock into a single knot around one key piece. The six-piece beech burr is the classic introduction; the twelve-piece walnut version adds a second layer of sticks and a far longer assembly.

Tangram sets with a pattern book

Seven maple pieces cut from one square: five triangles, a square and a parallelogram. The pattern book gives outlines only, so the dissection is yours to work out.

Sliding blocks in a tray

Rectangular blocks in a framed tray, one large block to walk to the exit slot. No lifting, no rotating; only sliding, and the order of moves is the whole puzzle.

Secret-opening puzzle boxes

Boxes that refuse to open until a sequence of hidden actions is found: a panel that shifts, a pin that drops, a drawer that only moves after something else has. Three-move and nine-move versions.

A grade and a solution card

Every puzzle carries its difficulty grade on the lid and a sealed card with the full solution inside. Open the envelope only when you choose to.

Wood chosen per puzzle

Beech, walnut and maple, each picked for stability, colour and how well it holds a crisp edge, then finished with a hard wax that keeps the sliding faces smooth.

How we work

From a plank to a sealed envelope

4E Logic is a small puzzle workshop in Bath. We make a short list of mechanical puzzles and make them carefully, rather than a long list made quickly. Boards of beech, walnut and maple are ripped into square stock, planed true and left to settle before a single notch is cut, because a stick that moves after cutting makes a burr that binds or rattles.

Each design is cut against jigs we built for it, then every piece is checked against its neighbours by hand. A burr is assembled and taken apart several times before it is boxed; a sliding tray is run through its full solution so we know no block catches; a puzzle box is opened and closed until the hidden actions feel deliberate rather than stiff.

When you order, we answer by e-mail, confirming the puzzles, the postage and the total, along with a link for paying securely. Your card is untouched unless you choose to follow it. When the money lands, the parcel is packed in recycled card with paper padding and goes out tracked.

What we value is the moment a puzzle gives way: the key piece sliding home, the last tangram triangle fitting the outline, the drawer finally moving. Everything about the grading and the sealed card is there to protect that moment, so you never stumble on the answer by accident.

The refusals are short and fixed. We make no toys for under-eights, no apps or digital puzzles, no custom engraving and no wholesale or trade supply. That keeps the workshop small and the puzzles consistent.

What does each grade mean?

We use four named grades rather than numbers. Warm-up opens or assembles in a sitting and teaches the idea behind the family. Steady needs a plan and a few false starts. Stubborn hides one move or one piece order that most people only find after putting it down and coming back. Fiendish is for solvers who already own a shelf of these and want a long evening. The grade is printed on the box lid and on the order page, so you always know what you are buying.

Is the solution included?

Yes. Every puzzle leaves the bench with a solution card in a sealed envelope taped inside the lid. Leave it shut and the puzzle stays honest; open it and you get a step-by-step sequence drawn in plain line diagrams, piece by piece or move by move. Lose it and we will e-mail a copy, free.

Which wood is best?

Beech is pale, close-grained and very stable, so it suits the six-piece burrs where tight joints matter. Walnut is darker and slightly softer to the touch, and we use it for the twelve-piece burr and the larger boxes because the colour shows the joints clearly. Maple is hard and bright and takes crisp edges, which is why the tangram pieces are cut from it. None is better in the abstract; each is picked for what the puzzle asks of it.

How long is delivery?

Puzzles in stock are packed and handed to the courier within two working days. UK mainland parcels usually arrive two to three working days after that; Ireland and the EU take longer. Costs and times for each region are listed on our delivery page.

Can I return a puzzle?

Yes, within thirty days of delivery, solved or not, as long as every piece comes back with it. The returns page explains who pays postage and how fast the refund lands.

Are these suitable for young children?

No. Our puzzles are made for ages 8 and up. The burr sticks and sliding blocks are small enough to be a hazard for little ones, and the thinking they ask for is beyond most under-eights anyway. We do not make toys for that age group at all.
