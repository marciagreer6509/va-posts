Insulation, honestly

How long does it stay cool?

That depends on the day more than on the bag: a car seat in a Houston August and an air-conditioned office are different worlds. So we do not print an hour figure. What we can tell you is how the bag is built: foam in all six walls, a zip that closes the top completely, and a mesh sleeve that holds a flat freezer pack against the lid where cold air does the most good.

Start with the bento box straight from the refrigerator and a frozen pack on top, and keep the bag out of direct sun.

Keeping it clean

Wipe the lining with a damp cloth and a drop of dish soap after any spill, then leave the bag open to air dry overnight. Once a month, or after a leak, hand-wash the whole bag in a sink of warm water, rinse, and dry it upside down with the zip open.

Do not put it in a washing machine or dryer; the tumbling crushes the foam and the heat can lift the taped seams. The shoulder strap unclips and can be washed on its own.

Packing order that works

Box flat on the bottom. Sauce pots upright beside it or inside the large compartment. Bottle along the long side. Freezer pack in the lid sleeve. Utensil case in the front pocket so you can reach it without unpacking. Packed this way, nothing rattles and the lid of the box never takes weight.

The bag is a single size on purpose: a lunch bag that is too big leaves air for the box to slide around in, and a bag that is too small presses on the clips.
