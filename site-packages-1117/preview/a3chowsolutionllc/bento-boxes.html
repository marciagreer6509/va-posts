Why steel

Stainless steel does not stain from tomato or turmeric, does not hold smells from yesterday's lunch, and does not crack if the box is dropped on a parking lot. 18/8 means the alloy carries 18 percent chromium and 8 percent nickel, the standard grade used for kitchen sinks and good cutlery.

How the seal works

Under each lid sits a single molded silicone gasket shaped like the compartments below it. When the four clips close, the lid presses that gasket down onto the top edge of every divider at once, so a juicy cell cannot seep into a dry one.

The seal is made to keep wet and dry portions apart, and to stop dressing or fruit juice reaching your bag. It is not designed for broth carried on its side; for anything thin, use a sauce pot and set it in the largest cell.

If the lid starts to feel loose, check the gasket first. It should sit flat in its channel with no twist. Press it back in along its length with a thumb, starting at one corner.

Cleaning and care

Steel bodies, cups and lids go in the dishwasher on any rack. Pull the silicone seal out first and wash it in warm, soapy water; that keeps it soft and stops it stretching in a hot drying cycle.

Dry everything before closing the box for storage. Leave the lid unclipped in the cupboard so the seal does not take a set. If a cell ever looks cloudy, a paste of baking soda and water lifts it without scratching.

Steel is not for the microwave. Move portions to a plate or to one of our glass containers if you need to reheat at work.

Choosing between the two

Pick the three-cell box if your lunch is one main portion with a couple of sides. Pick the five-cell box if you like small portions of many things, or if you want the option to lift two cups out and make room for a sandwich. Both fit the insulated lunch bag lying flat.
