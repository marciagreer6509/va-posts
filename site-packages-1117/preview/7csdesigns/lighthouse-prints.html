Bearing 015°The series

A series of headland lights

The lighthouse prints are a series rather than a set of separate pictures. Each sheet is drawn to the same tall format, with the tower standing on its headland or rock in the lower half and the sea opening out above it. Hung in a row, the sheets read like pages from one coastal pilot book, and that is how they are meant to be seen. Most people start with one light they know well and add a neighbour later.

A lighthouse print is drawn differently from a harbour. The tower itself is shown in elevation, as a small exact drawing with its gallery, lantern and keepers' cottages, set above a plan of the rocks around it. Arcs on the water mark the light's sectors as a chart would show them, and a bearing line runs out to sea from the tower with the clearing mark lettered along it. Soundings in fathoms fill the water around the rocks, close-set where it shoals.

We add the character of the light in the old shorthand, the way it would be printed beside the tower on a chart, because it is part of the poetry of these places. As with every sheet we make, it is there to be looked at, not relied upon: the prints are decorative and must not be used for any decision at sea.

Bearing 080°Sizes & prices

Lighthouse print sizes and prices

Because the lighthouse sheets are tall, A3 and A2 suit them best: the tower keeps its detail and the sea above it has room to breathe. A4 works well for a pair either side of a window or on a narrow wall in a stairwell. All three standard colourways are available, and you can choose your own two inks for any light at no extra cost.
