Keeping your details on a short line

A print studio needs very little from you: enough to make a sheet, post it and answer your questions. This notice sets out exactly what 7Cs Designs gathers through 7csdesigns.com, the use it is put to, and the say you keep over it. The studio sits in Falmouth, Cornwall, England; questions about your information go to prints@7csdesigns.com, where they are read by the studio itself.

What arrives when an order form is sent

Pressing send hands us the name and mailbox you typed, the postal address for the parcel, your chosen place, size, colourway and frame, and whatever you wrote in the message box. FormSubmit, a forwarding service, carries those fields from the page into the studio inbox. From there they serve four purposes only: writing back, issuing the payment link, making and posting the print, and handling a return should one come back. Order paperwork stays on file for six years, which UK tax rules ask of any trader; general letters are cleared out after two years at most.

Cookies, and the little banner in the corner

No cookie is written by these pages. When you answer the banner, your browser holds a single note under the key sevc_cookie, purely so the question is not put to you twice. Today that note goes unread, because the site carries no measuring, advertising or tracking code at all — and if any were ever added, it would stay switched off for anyone who had not said yes. Apart from the two typefaces, which load from a font host, a page here makes no request beyond its own address.

Who else sees an order

Your details are never sold, hired out or swapped. Three parties touch an order besides the studio: the forwarding service that delivers the form, whichever payment provider handles the link you pay through, and the carrier that drops the parcel at your door.

The say you keep

UK and EU data protection law lets you request a full copy of the records kept about you, put right anything wrong in it, have it erased, take it away in a portable file, or object to a particular use. Residents of California hold equivalent rights under state law: to learn what is gathered, to have it erased, to refuse its sale — though nothing is sold here — and to receive the same treatment whichever of those rights they use. Send any such request to prints@7csdesigns.com; a full answer follows inside a calendar month. If our reply falls short, you may take the matter to the Information Commissioner's Office, the UK regulator for personal data.
