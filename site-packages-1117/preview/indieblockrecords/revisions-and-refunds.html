Revisions and refunds

Deposits, stages, what comes back if you cancel, how many rounds of changes are included, and what happens if the studio cannot finish.

The deposit

Every booking starts with a deposit of half the agreed total, paid through the secure payment link in our reply email. No payment is taken before that link, and no work starts before the deposit is in. The other half is due when you approve the final mix or master, before the final WAV files are delivered.

What each stage costs, and what comes back

  • Booked, files not yet opened. Cancel any time before we start work and the whole deposit is refunded.
  • Files checked, first mix or master not yet delivered. Cancel and the whole deposit is refunded, minus nothing: reading files is part of booking, not a billable step.
  • First mix or master delivered. From this point the deposit for each song we have worked on is earned. If you cancel, there is no further charge for those songs, and the deposit for any song we have not started is refunded in full.
  • Approved. Once you approve a song, its balance is due and the final files are yours on payment.

On an EP bundle, songs are counted one by one in the same way: the bundle price is split evenly across its five songs for the purpose of any refund.

Revision limits

Each mix and each master includes two rounds of notes. A round is one message holding all of your changes. A third round, or a change that needs new parts — a fresh vocal take, a replaced bass — is quoted by email before we touch it, and nothing is billed without your written yes.

When the studio is the one that falls short

Should we fail to finish a song for any reason on our side — the files cannot make a mix we would stand behind, or we cannot meet the turnaround and you would rather not wait — every payment for that unfinished song is refunded in full.

How refunds are made

Ask by email to mix@indieblockrecords.com. Refunds go back to the method you paid with, within five working days of the request being agreed. Card statements sometimes lag behind that by a handful of days; that delay sits with the card issuer, not with the studio.
