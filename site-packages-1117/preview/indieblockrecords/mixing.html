Mixing, per track, from your stems

You send the parts, we build the mix, you give notes. Here is what to send, how the notes work, how long each step takes and what it costs.

What to send

A mix can only be as good as the parts that arrive, so the first thing we do with any song is read the files. Send one WAV per track, 24-bit, at the sample rate the song was recorded in. Every stem starts at the very top of the song, even the shaker that only plays in the last chorus, so the whole session lines up the moment it is dropped in. Name files by what they are: lead vocal, double left, double right, kick in, kick out, bass DI, bass amp.

  • Dry and wet, both. If a delay throw or a guitar pedal is part of the sound, print it as its own stem beside the dry one. We can keep yours, rebuild it, or blend the two.
  • Nothing on the mix bus. Switch off any limiter or bus compression before you export. Leave a few decibels of headroom on every stem; nothing should touch zero.
  • A rough mix. Your rough is the brief. It tells us where the vocal sat when you fell for the song, and that is worth more than a page of adjectives.
  • A reference or two. Name a released song whose low end or vocal space you like. We listen for the specific thing you point at, not for a copy.

How the mix gets built

Every session starts with static balance: faders only, no plugins, until the song plays itself. Then the bus structure goes in — drums to one bus, music to another, vocals to their own — so compression and EQ can glue groups together instead of fighting on every channel. Automation comes last, riding the vocal word by word where it needs it. The mixdown that reaches you is printed with headroom left for mastering, never squashed to sound impressive on first play.

How notes work

Two rounds of notes are included in every mix price. A round is one message with everything you want changed, so collect thoughts from the band first and send them together. The most useful notes have a timestamp and a direction: the snare at the bridge is too bright, the lead vocal disappears under the guitars from the second verse, the reverb tail on the last word is too long. Vague notes are fine too — we answer them with a question rather than a guess.

Notes that change the song itself, such as a new vocal take or a re-recorded bass part, start a fresh mix of that song, and we quote it before touching it. Notes about balance, tone, space and level are exactly what the two rounds are for.

Turnaround

For a single song, the first mix goes out within seven working days of the stems and the deposit arriving. A five-song EP takes up to fifteen working days for its first round, because we mix the songs side by side so they sit together. Each round of notes comes back within three working days. If your files need fixing first — a missing part, a stem that drifts — the clock starts once the corrected files are in.

What you get back

Previews come as streaming links for listening on your own speakers, earbuds and car. After approval and the final payment, you receive the mixdown as a WAV 24-bit file at the session rate, ready for our mastering or anyone else's. Instrumental and clean versions are printed from the same approved balance if you add them.

What mixing here does not include

We do not write parts, hire session musicians, or tune and edit whole performances from scratch. Light timing fixes and a few tuning touches on exposed notes are part of a normal mix; rebuilding a performance is not. Uploading the finished song to stores, pitching it to playlists and handling royalties all stay with you.
