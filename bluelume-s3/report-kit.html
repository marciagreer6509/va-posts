Open-bucket good-samaritan alert kit

Find misconfigured Amazon S3 buckets (read-only, legal), identify the owner, and warn them. Never upload a test file — that write is the one illegal step, and you don't need it.

Part 1 — Find similar buckets (all read-only)

  1. Public index — easiest. Go to grayhatwarfare.com (free account). It already indexes millions of open buckets. Search a keyword (a city, a vendor like cms, prod, static-files) and it lists open buckets and their files. Browsing is legal — the files are already public.
  2. Name-pattern scan. Buckets follow patterns: <org>-files, <org>-prod, <org>-static, <vendor>-us-prod-app-<client>. Open-source tools s3scanner, bucket_finder, cloud_enum take a wordlist of org names and test each name. A bucket that answers (not "NoSuchBucket") exists.
  3. Read the default response. Hitting a bucket you don't own returns one of: NoSuchBucket (free/available), AccessDenied (exists, locked), or a file listing (exists, public-read). Reading these is passive and fine.
  4. Spot the already-abused ones. If a bucket is already serving pages the owner clearly didn't make (spam, redirects, foreign-language affiliate pages), that is itself proof the bucket is open — no test upload needed.
  5. 🛑 The one line you don't cross: do not upload a file to confirm writability. That write is unauthorized access. Report what you can already see instead.

Part 2 — Find the owner & where to report

Fastest route for ALL of them is AWS Abuse — Amazon knows who owns each bucket and can act. Notify the org directly too when they publish a contact.

Part 3 — Copy-paste emails

✉️ A — to the bucket owner
Subject: Security notice — your Amazon S3 bucket is publicly writable and hosting third-party pages

Hello,

I'm a member of the public who came across what looks like a
misconfiguration in your web hosting, and I wanted to flag it to you
as a courtesy. I have not changed, downloaded in bulk, or interfered
with anything.

Your Amazon S3 bucket "BUCKET-NAME-HERE" is publicly readable and
appears to be publicly writable. It is currently serving web pages
that I don't believe your organisation created — for example:

  https://BUCKET-NAME-HERE.s3.REGION.amazonaws.com/path/to/page.html

These pages are keyword-stuffed "review" pages that redirect ordinary
visitors to unrelated affiliate shopping offers, while showing search
engines plain text so they get indexed. In other words, someone is
using your storage to host spam.

Why it matters:
 • Anyone on the internet can currently upload files into this bucket.
 • Google may associate this spam (and any future malicious content)
   with your domain, which can hurt your search reputation.
 • You are paying Amazon to store and serve someone else's content.

Suggested steps (for your IT/security team):
 1. In the S3 console, turn ON "Block all public access" for the bucket,
    or tighten the bucket policy / ACL so only your accounts can write.
 2. Review the object list and remove files your team didn't upload.
 3. If any access keys were shared or committed anywhere, rotate them.

I'm not seeking anything in return — just passing this along so you can
close it. Happy to share the specific URLs I saw if useful.

Kind regards,
YOUR NAME
