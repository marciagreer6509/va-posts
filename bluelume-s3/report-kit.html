Open-bucket alert kit

Find misconfigured Amazon S3 buckets with public tools, confirm the owner, and send them a professional heads-up.

Part 1 — Find the buckets

  1. Public index (start here). Open grayhatwarfare.com and make a free account. It already catalogues millions of open buckets and their files. Search a keyword — a city, a company, or a pattern like static-files, cms, prod, uploads — and it returns open buckets you can browse straight away.
  2. Scan by name pattern. Buckets are named predictably: <org>-files, <org>-prod, <org>-static, <org>-uploads, <vendor>-us-prod-app-<client>. Feed a list of organisation names into an open-source scanner and let it check each guess:
    # install & run s3scanner against a list of names pip install s3scanner s3scanner scan --bucket-file names.txt
  3. Read the reply to know what you found:
    • NoSuchBucket → the name is free / unused.
    • AccessDenied → the bucket exists but is locked down.
    • an XML file listing → the bucket is public; you can see its contents.
    You can check any bucket's listing directly in a browser: https://<bucket>.s3.amazonaws.com/?list-type=2
  4. Confirm it's being abused. If the bucket is already serving pages the owner clearly didn't make — spam "review" pages, redirects, foreign-language affiliate pages — that's your evidence it's wide open. Note one or two example URLs to put in the email.
  5. Identify the owner. The bucket name and the legitimate files usually name the organisation. Confirm with their website, a WHOIS on their domain, or https://<org>.com/.well-known/security.txt.

Part 3 — The emails

✉️ A — to the bucket owner
Subject: Heads-up — your Amazon S3 bucket is open and serving pages you didn't publish

Hello,

I came across what looks like a misconfiguration in your web hosting
and wanted to flag it as a courtesy.

Your Amazon S3 bucket "BUCKET-NAME-HERE" is publicly accessible and is
currently serving web pages that don't appear to be yours — for example:

  https://BUCKET-NAME-HERE.s3.REGION.amazonaws.com/path/to/page.html

These are keyword-stuffed "review" pages that redirect visitors to
unrelated affiliate shopping offers, while showing search engines plain
text so they get indexed. In short, someone is using your storage to
host spam.

Why it's worth fixing quickly:
 • The bucket accepts uploads from outside your organisation.
 • Google can associate this spam with your domain and hurt your
   search reputation.
 • You're paying Amazon to store and serve someone else's content.

Suggested steps for your IT team:
 1. In the S3 console, turn ON "Block all public access" for the bucket,
    or tighten the bucket policy / ACL so only your accounts can write.
 2. Review the object list and delete files your team didn't upload.
 3. Rotate any access keys that may have been exposed.

I'm not after anything in return — just passing it along so you can
close it. Happy to share the exact URLs I saw if that helps.

Kind regards,
YOUR NAME
