How the BlueLume S3 network works

Checked 12–13 Nov 2026 from the 42 live pages, their code, DNS/whois, and the first redirect hop of each cloak (never followed further). Facts are marked proven; inferences are marked likely.

Short answer: one affiliate account is behind at least 6 of the 7 buckets, possibly all 7. It writes throw-away "video review" pages into other organisations' Amazon S3 storage, shows Google a text page with a video thumbnail, and sends every real visitor to an affiliate link. The 26-minute "video" is one still image. Google ranks it because the brand is new (almost nothing else answers "bluelume glasses review"), because a video result gets a big thumbnail, and because the cloak only runs for humans.

4. Why Google ranks this junk

ReasonWhy it works
Empty search results for new brandsBlueLume launched weeks ago. For "bluelume glasses review" Google has very few pages to choose from, so any exact-match page with the full phrase in the title, H1, URL and text gets in. The same reason a fresh brand page of ours can rank in days.
Video result = a big thumbnailA VideoObject with a real, playable MP4 qualifies the page for video results. Google checks that a video exists and that it is the page's main item; it doesn't judge whether 26 minutes of a still image is a real video. The clickbait thumbnail plus the duration badge pull clicks.
The cloak only fires for humansThe redirect is JavaScript that checks for "Googlebot" in the user agent, so Google's crawler and renderer never see it fire. Google does spot-check with other user agents, but that's after the fact, and the pages are disposable.
Borrowed, clean hosts*.s3.amazonaws.com buckets of real organisations have no spam history. Each bucket is a fresh hostname that hasn't been penalised, and burning one costs the spammer nothing.
Freshness + volumeNew pages every few hours win "hours ago" freshness on brand queries. Hundreds of spun variants mean some survive each Google clean-up.
Google is slow, not blindThis breaks 4 Google spam policies at once: cloaking, sneaky redirects, scaled content abuse, and hacked/third-party content. SpamBrain and manual actions remove it, usually days to weeks later. Short-lived offers make those days pay.

5. How they write to other people's buckets

not tested The buckets refuse public listing, so this can't be checked from outside without trying to write into them, which is illegal and I didn't do it. The usual causes are a bucket left open to public uploads, or AWS keys leaked in a public code repository or app. The owners (a city, a university, charities) almost certainly don't know. AWS has an abuse form for exactly this, and the owner can delete the files in minutes.

6. What it means for us (clean lessons only)

  • The prize is real: brand + "review" searches for a new offer have almost no competition in the first weeks. Exact-match pages on day 0 win them (our best-seo / offer-seo rule, confirmed again).
  • Video results take the top of the page: a genuine video (real footage, real content) on our own pages, with VideoObject schema and a matching thumbnail, can win the same Videos block, legitimately.
  • Don't copy the cloak: a UA-based redirect and pages on hijacked buckets break Google's spam policies and computer-misuse law. A manual action would hit the whole domain.
  • If it steals our sales: a Google spam report (cloaking) plus an AWS abuse report per bucket takes the pages down fast. Telling the bucket owners works too (e.g. the City of Newport Beach).
